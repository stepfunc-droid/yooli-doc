# Yooli Asset-Side and Guarantee-System Contraction Timeline

> Updated: 2026-10-16  
> Chinese original: [`有利网资产端与担保体系风险收缩时间线.md`](../docs/有利网资产端与担保体系风险收缩时间线.md)  
> This English version is a translation for research use. If wording differs, the Chinese original controls.
>
> This article places verified events involving asset-side institutions, guarantee companies, factoring companies, and Yooli itself on a single timeline. The purpose is not to presuppose “coordinated asset transfer,” but to answer a more basic question: **before Yooli’s repayment crisis became public in mid-2020, had its asset-side and guarantee/compensation system already shown a sequence of contraction, capital reduction, liquidation, judicial auction, or judicial abnormality?**
>
> **Evidentiary boundary:** temporal proximity does not establish causation. The events below come separately from regulatory announcements, listed-company disclosures, newspaper notices, court decisions, and public platform/media responses. Without cash-flow records, internal meeting materials, or regulatory disposition documents, they do not justify a conclusion that these actions were coordinated.

## 1. 2018–2019: underlying defaults were already being transferred outward through guarantee compensation

Public court decisions show that in Puhui Kuaixin consumer loans originated in 2018, when borrowers became delinquent Yooli’s operating company would issue a compensation notice to a financing-guarantee company. The guarantor would compensate Honghe Boji and then obtain a recovery claim against the borrower.

Examples:

- **Cheng Bin:** delinquency began 2018-09-27; Guoyin Shengda compensated Honghe Boji on **2019-06-17**;
- **Huang Panpan:** delinquency began 2018-09-15; Guoyin Shengda compensated RMB 18,547.27 on **2019-07-29**;
- **Chen Changhe:** on **2018-11-13**, Honghe Boji required Chengxixiang to compensate, and Chengxixiang transferred funds to Honghe Boji’s Sichuan branch the same day.

These cases show that whether a lender-facing account was “overdue” and whether the underlying borrower had defaulted were different questions: **underlying borrower default could first be absorbed by a guarantor while lender payments remained normal.**

Sources:

- Guoyin Shengda v. Cheng Bin:  
  https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/安徽省桐城市人民法院（2022）皖0881民初798号民事判决书
- Guoyin Shengda v. Huang Panpan:  
  [《广西壮族自治区靖西市人民法院（2023）桂1081民初3928号民事判决书》](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/广西壮族自治区靖西市人民法院（2023）桂1081民初3928号民事判决书)
- Chengxixiang v. Chen Changhe:  
  [《陈昌和、深圳市诚信祥融资担保有限公司追偿权纠纷民事二审民事判决书》（2020）琼96民终2868号](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/陈昌和、深圳市诚信祥融资担保有限公司追偿权纠纷民事二审民事判决书)

This is consistent with the project’s separate finding that in 2019 Yooli could show “zero overdue” while cumulative compensation continued rising. A material share of underlying risk may initially have accumulated in asset-side and guarantee institutions rather than appearing immediately as lender-side delinquency.

### A contemporaneous quantified example: roughly RMB 93 million remained outstanding in the Hemei channel

Hemei Group’s annual reports repeatedly disclosed that its controlled Hemei Microcredit and Hemei Zhike had signed an asset/guarantee cooperation agreement with Yooli with a **RMB 500 million cooperation scale**. At the end of 2018, the Yooli-channel “outstanding loan balance” was still **RMB 93,209,736.10**; at the end of 2019 it was **RMB 92,631,610.53**.

Sources:

- Hemei Group 2018 annual report:  
  https://money.finance.sina.com.cn/corp/view/vCB_AllBulletinDetail.php?id=5334678&stockid=002356
- Hemei Group 2019 annual report:  
  https://vip.stock.finance.sina.com.cn/corp/view/vCB_AllBulletinDetail.php?id=6329959&stockid=002356
- Hemei Group response concerning compensation-liability contracts:  
  https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2019-09-27/1206952595.PDF

This shows that Yooli’s asset side did not depend only on Puhui Kuaixin, Lixin, and a few other institutions. At least some remaining assets had been sourced, serviced, and jointly guaranteed by financial subsidiaries of a listed company.

## 2. 2020-01-14: Chengxixiang still obtained a renewed financing-guarantee license

The Shenzhen local financial regulator announced the first group of renewed financing-guarantee business licenses. **Shenzhen Chengxixiang Financing Guarantee Co., Ltd.** met the renewal conditions and received license **Yue (Shenzhen) A0006**.

Source:

- Shenzhen Local Financial Supervision and Administration Bureau:  
  https://jr.sz.gov.cn/gkmlpt/content/6/6926/post_6926944.html

At least at the administrative-licensing level in January 2020, Chengxixiang was still being treated as a financing-guarantee institution permitted to continue licensed operations.

## 3. 2020-02-21: Guoyin Shengda was approved to reduce registered capital from RMB 700 million to RMB 300 million

The Shenzhen local financial regulator formally approved a capital reduction by **Shenzhen Guoyin Shengda Financing Guarantee Co., Ltd.**:

- original registered capital: **RMB 700 million**;
- post-reduction capital: **RMB 300 million**;
- business address: Unit 01, 8/F, Rongchao Tower, 4036 Jintian Road, Futian District, Shenzhen.

Source:

- Shenzhen Local Financial Supervision and Administration Bureau:  
  https://jr.sz.gov.cn/sjrb/xxgk/tzgg/content/post_6926024.html

Guoyin Shengda was not an unrelated guarantor. Multiple judgments confirm that it provided joint and several guarantees for **Puhui Kuaixin consumer loans referred to Yooli** and obtained recovery rights after compensation.

The reduction therefore occurred at a key Yooli guarantee node and is worth recording. There is currently no evidence, however, that the capital reduction itself was undertaken to evade Yooli-related compensation obligations.

## 4. 2020-04-03: Puhui Kuaixin reduced registered capital from RMB 100 million to RMB 30 million

*New Express Daily* published a capital-reduction notice for **Shenzhen Puhui Kuaixin Financial Services Co., Ltd.**:

- registered capital: **RMB 100 million → RMB 30 million**;
- creditors were asked to complete relevant procedures within 45 days of the notice;
- the notice expressly said the company would remain liable for debts within the original registered-capital amount and shareholders would bear joint liability.

Source:

- *New Express Daily*, 2020-04-03:  
  https://ep.ycwb.com/epaper/xkb/html/2020-04/03/content_318_251489.htm

Puhui Kuaixin is a core Yooli consumer-finance asset-side institution repeatedly confirmed by court judgments, so this capital reduction is more significant than a reduction by a peripheral entity.

The current record still does not establish that Yooli continued to control Puhui Kuaixin through equity or contract in 2020, and temporal proximity alone cannot justify characterizing the reduction as asset transfer by the Yooli system.

## 5. 2020-04-24: two different systems showed abnormal events on the same day

### 5.1 Shenzhen Honghe Boji Commercial Factoring entered liquidation

A liquidation notice in *New Express Daily* stated that **Shenzhen Honghe Boji Commercial Factoring Co., Ltd.** had resolved to cease business and deregister, and had formed a liquidation group whose members included:

- Han Guangshan;
- **Beijing Honghe Boji Financial Information Service Co., Ltd. (Yooli’s operating entity)**.

Source:

- *New Express Daily*, 2020-04-24:  
  https://ep.ycwb.com/epaper/xkb/h5/html5/2020-04/24/content_321_259134.htm

This factoring company was directly invested in by Yooli’s operating entity. The liquidation report has not been obtained, so the amount of receivables, cash, or other assets then remaining—and the distribution of residual property—are unknown.

### 5.2 Huai’an Intermediate Court upheld transfer of several Chengxixiang recovery cases to public-security authorities

On the same day, Huai’an Intermediate People’s Court in Jiangsu handled a group of retrial cases involving Chengxixiang recovery claims, including:

- (2020) Su 08 Min Zai No. 115 — Zhu Tingting;
- (2020) Su 08 Min Zai No. 116 — Zhao Meng;
- (2020) Su 08 Min Zai No. 117 — Zhang Weiyang;
- (2020) Su 08 Min Zai No. 118 — Yan Zhaowu;
- and (2020) Su 08 Min Zai No. 87 — Qian Nanjing.

The common procedural result was:

> prior effective civil judgments vacated → Chengxixiang’s suits dismissed → **the entire matters transferred to public-security authorities for investigation**.

The retrial court used the wording that **“Chengxixiang may be suspected of a crime.”**

Sources:

- https://www.tingsonglaw.com/document?doctypeno=2&fornext=ae173e8abd8879f8a9bbab567957d2b9&p=177
- https://www.tingsonglaw.com/document?doctypeno=2&fornext=e1c450b6fd52bf00871f3e5cb78faa89&p=176

The public decisions do not identify the suspected offense, and no subsequent public-security filing or investigation result has been located. The evidence therefore supports only “multiple cases transferred to public-security authorities,” not a conclusion that “Chengxixiang committed a crime.”

### Why this date is worth marking separately

On 2020-04-24, two things occurred:

- Yooli’s directly invested commercial-factoring company entered liquidation;
- a group of historical recovery cases involving a core guarantor for Yooli consumer loans was transferred to public-security authorities because the court said the guarantor “may be suspected of a crime.”

There is no evidence establishing a connection between the two. On the broader risk timeline, however, this is a clear **asset-side / guarantee-side contraction and judicial-abnormality point**.

## 6. May 2020: 51% of Hemei Zhike was judicially auctioned and transferred

A separate external asset/guarantee system also underwent a major change.

Hemei Group disclosed that, because of a contract dispute with Huzhou Shenghua Financial Services Co., Ltd., a court judicially auctioned the listed company’s **51% equity interest in Shenzhen Hemei Zhihui Technology Co., Ltd. (Hemei Zhike)**. Shenzhen Boguan Investment Development Co., Ltd. won the interest for **RMB 2 million**.

- corporate-registration change completed 2020-05-09;
- equity delivery completed 2020-05-31;
- Hemei Group thereafter no longer controlled Hemei Zhike and ceased consolidating it.

Hemei Group’s 2020 annual report also disclosed that Hemei Zhike had net assets of approximately **negative RMB 397 million** at disposal.

Sources:

- Hemei Group 2020 annual report and change in consolidation scope:  
  https://epaper.cs.com.cn/zgzqb/images/2021-04/30/B283/zqB28330.pdf
- corrected judicial-auction disclosure:  
  https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2020-03-24/f0ca7a7e-77e6-4085-b448-168bb04db4ca.PDF

Hemei Zhike was not unrelated to Yooli. Under a cooperation agreement signed in late 2014, it and Hemei Microcredit jointly assumed guarantee obligations for Yooli-channel loans. As of 2020-06-30, Hemei Group’s financial statements still showed **RMB 92,322,033.58** in Yooli-channel outstanding loans.

Source:

- Hemei Group 2020 interim report:  
  https://vip.stock.finance.sina.com.cn/corp/view/vCB_AllBulletinDetail.php?id=6578594&stockid=002356

This means that **one month before Yooli stopped new loan disbursements, at least one important historical guarantor in an external partner system had been judicially auctioned because of its parent group’s debt crisis and had left the original control structure.**

This does not establish that the Hemei Zhike auction caused Yooli’s crisis, but it plainly weakened the practical credit-enhancement capacity of that historical cooperation chain.

## 7. 2020-06-30: Yooli stopped new loan disbursements

In July 2020, Yooli confirmed to media that the platform had **stopped new loan disbursements on 2020-06-30**.

Sources:

- Nanfang Metropolis Daily / Nanfang+:  
  https://static.nfnews.com/content/202007/24/c3810968.html
- Securities Times:  
  https://news.stcn.com/sd/202007/t20200725_2166328.html

The platform later continued to use the same “new listings stopped on 2020-06-30” wording in responses to user complaints.

This date indicates that the previous rolling system of “old-loan repayment/compensation → rematching into assets → exit through claim transfer” had begun to move into a true run-off phase.

---

## 8. July 2020: overdue amounts and claim-transfer problems became publicly acute

Media reporting in July 2020 focused heavily on lenders being unable to exit at the end of their service periods. Public figures then showed:

- overdue amount at end-June of about **RMB 2.678 billion**;
- an increase of about **RMB 1.05 billion** from end-May;
- about **1.379 million borrowers**;
- materially slower claim transfers.

Yooli customer service publicly explained that COVID had reduced borrower repayment capacity. It also said that if borrowers referred by partner institutions did not meet contractual requirements, those partners would **repurchase and compensate** the relevant assets.

Sources:

- Nanfang+:  
  https://static.nfnews.com/content/202007/25/c3813683.html
- Securities Times:  
  https://news.stcn.com/sd/202007/t20200725_2166328.html

This statement matters because, even after the crisis became public, Yooli still acknowledged **partner-institution repurchase/compensation** as one mechanism for handling certain problem assets.

## 9. December 2020: 51% of Hemei Microcredit was also judicially auctioned for RMB 2 million

Another core financial subsidiary of Hemei Group, **Shenzhen Hemei Microcredit Co., Ltd.**, was also subjected to judicial disposition.

On 2020-12-04, Hemei Group’s 51% interest in Hemei Microcredit was won at a Shenzhen Intermediate Court judicial auction by Yangjiang Afanti Technology Co., Ltd. for **RMB 2 million**. The listed company later disclosed that because of changes in enforcement procedure, there were later disputes concerning cancellation / non-completion of the auction, so it should not simply be stated that final transfer was completed.

Hemei Group’s own description of Hemei Microcredit’s business is important. It said the company:

> developed customers for multiple funding channels, reviewed loan materials, accepted mandates to handle loan disbursement, day-to-day management and recovery, collected handling/service fees from borrowers, and assumed compensation liability for loans funded through multiple capital channels.

Source:

- Hemei Group announcement and response concerning the judicial auction of 51% of Hemei Microcredit:  
  https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2021-01-05/doc-iiznezxt0585862.shtml

More notably, Hemei Group still disclosed Yooli-channel outstanding loan balances of **RMB 92,111,793.55** at end-2020 and **RMB 91,700,464.69** in mid-2021.

Sources:

- Hemei Group 2020 annual report:  
  https://vip.stock.finance.sina.com.cn/corp/view/vCB_AllBulletinDetail.php?id=7372909&stockid=002356
- Hemei Group 2021 interim report:  
  https://vip.stock.finance.sina.com.cn/corp/view/vCB_AllBulletinDetail.php?id=7513599&stockid=002356

Thus, **the historical Yooli loan balance generated through the Hemei cooperation did not disappear quickly merely because the cooperation partner itself fell into severe financial distress.** It remained around RMB 92 million from 2018 through 2021.

## 10. February 2021: Chengxixiang’s 2019 annual regulatory review still “passed”

On **2021-02-26**, the Shenzhen local financial regulator published 2019 annual-review results for financing-guarantee companies. Chengxixiang appeared in the first batch with a result of **“annual review passed.”**

Source:

- Shenzhen Local Financial Supervision and Administration Bureau:  
  https://jr.sz.gov.cn/sjrb/ydmh/isz/wgk/content/post_8691867.html

This is not inconsistent with Huai’an courts transferring Chengxixiang cases to public-security authorities in April 2020, but it establishes an important point:

> **administrative licensing / industry annual review and a court’s assessment that particular recovery transactions may involve suspected criminal conduct are different systems of review.**

The annual-review result cannot negate the judicial transfers, and the judicial transfers cannot be used to infer that Shenzhen regulators had already found Chengxixiang criminally unlawful.

## 11. March 2021: Guoyin Shengda became subject to a formal regulatory risk warning

On 2021-03-22/23, the Shenzhen local financial regulator announced that:

- Guoyin Shengda’s financing-guarantee license had been valid through **2020-09-17**;
- actual controller Li Baoxiang had been listed by courts as a **dishonest judgment debtor and subject to consumption restrictions**;
- relevant institutions and individuals were advised to conduct business cautiously.

Source:

- Shenzhen Local Financial Supervision and Administration Bureau:  
  https://jr.sz.gov.cn/sjrb/xxgk/tzgg/content/post_8644310.html

Notably, the notice still described Guoyin Shengda as having “registered capital of RMB 700 million,” even though the regulator had approved a reduction to RMB 300 million in February 2020. Possible explanations include incomplete corporate-registration implementation, different data definitions, or use of outdated information in the notice. Current materials are insufficient to determine the exact reason.

## 12. What is the most reasonable interpretation when these events are viewed together?

The verified record supports a fuller process than the simplistic explanation that “COVID suddenly made all loans bad in 2020”:

```text
2018–2019
many borrowers already delinquent
        ↓
Chengxixiang / Guoyin Shengda / Hemei and other systems guarantee or compensate
lender-facing risk temporarily absorbed
        ↓
2020-02 Guoyin Shengda major capital reduction
2020-04 Puhui Kuaixin capital reduction
2020-04 Honghe Boji Commercial Factoring liquidation
2020-04 group of Chengxixiang recovery cases retried and transferred to police
2020-05 Hemei Zhike judicially auctioned and leaves original parent control
        ↓
2020-06 Yooli stops new loan disbursements
        ↓
2020-07 lender-side overdue and claim-transfer difficulty become acute
        ↓
2020-12 Hemei Microcredit equity enters judicial auction
2021-03 formal regulatory risk warning concerning Guoyin Shengda
```

This sequence supports a hypothesis worth further testing:

> **One important foundation of Yooli’s previous ability to maintain low or zero lender-side overdue was the compensation capacity of asset partners and financing-guarantee companies. Around 2020, not only did underlying borrowers deteriorate; the broader asset-side / guarantee-side system absorbing that deterioration may also have contracted at the same time, allowing risk previously absorbed outside the lender-facing layer to pass directly through to lenders.**

The Hemei example makes the hypothesis more concrete. This was not simply “one guarantor paid less”: a cooperation system that once had a **RMB 500 million scale** and still had around **RMB 92 million** outstanding in 2020 itself experienced a severe debt crisis, judicial auction of subsidiary shares, and changes of control during the same period.

This remains an **analytical hypothesis derived from timing and contractual structure**, not a proven single causal relationship.

To establish it more firmly, the following would be needed:

1. monthly actual compensation amounts by guarantor during 2018–2020;
2. guarantee balances and compensation limits for Chengxixiang, Guoyin Shengda, Hemei Microcredit/Hemei Zhike within the Yooli system;
3. identification of which partners stopped or reduced compensation around 2020;
4. the asset-source composition of Yooli’s surge in overdue amount;
5. whether capital reductions, liquidations, and judicial auctions occurred while unperformed guarantee/repurchase/compensation obligations remained outstanding;
6. whether Yooli actually enforced guarantee obligations when partners failed to compensate, how much it pursued, and how much it ultimately recovered.

Until those data appear, this article only places verified public events on the same timeline; it does not characterize them as asset transfer or coordinated unlawful conduct.
