# Yooli’s Self-Built / Controlled Asset-Side Operations: Evidence Chain and Evidentiary Boundaries

> Updated: 2026-10-16  
> Chinese original: [`有利网自建资产端证据链与证据边界.md`](../docs/有利网自建资产端证据链与证据边界.md)  
> This article organizes public materials concerning Yooli’s evolution from external asset partnerships toward deeper involvement in loan origination and servicing. The body first records management statements, court decisions, corporate / listed-company materials, and actual business division of labor; analytical conclusions about whether this amounted to a self-built or controlled asset side are reserved for the end.  
> **Evidentiary boundary:** “the asset side was built or controlled in part by the Yooli system” is not the same proposition as “self-financing.” The first asks who sourced borrowers, performed underwriting, serviced loans, provided guarantees, held collateral, and collected. The second requires evidence that lender funds ultimately financed the platform, its shareholders, actual controllers, or related parties themselves.  
> This English version is a research translation. If wording differs, the Chinese original controls.

---

## 1. From around 2015, management publicly described an expansion into the asset side

From around 2015 onward, multiple financial-media reports described a change in Yooli’s business strategy.

A Yicai article on Yooli’s restructuring said that, in the platform’s early phase, different founders were responsible respectively for resources, online traffic, and offline assets, with Wu Yiran long responsible for the asset side. As scale increased, Yooli began describing a shift away from being a purely “light” platform toward deeper asset-side involvement.

Source:

- Yicai, *Behind Yooli’s Restructuring: The Collective Growing Pains of China’s P2P Industry*  
  https://www.yicai.com/news/4683714.html

This source establishes that **moving deeper into the asset side was an explicit strategic direction publicly discussed by Yooli management.** By itself, it does not prove the legal control structure of any particular asset-side company.

---

## 2. Late 2015 to early 2016: public reports used terms such as “asset-side acquisitions” and “credit factory”

From late 2015 into early 2016, financial media reporting on personnel and business changes at Lixin Kuaijie, Puhui Kuaixin, and other asset-side institutions quoted Yooli or related persons as saying that:

- Yooli had completed acquisitions or integration of several asset-side institutions;
- Shenzhen Lixin Kuaijie focused primarily on small credit loans;
- Shenzhen Puhui Kuaixin focused primarily on consumer installments;
- Yooli intended to build a consumer-finance “credit factory.”

Sources:

- *Huaxia Times* / Sina Finance, *Yooli Recently Acquires Several Asset-Side Institutions, Aiming to Build a “Credit Factory”*  
  https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2016-01-30/doc-ifxnzanh0361192.shtml
- *Economic Observer*, *Wu Yiran: Asset-Side Acquisitions and Integration Completed; Building a Consumer-Finance Credit Factory*  
  https://www.eeo.com.cn/2016/0109/282371.shtml
- *China Business Journal* / Phoenix Tech, *Competing for People and Assets: Yooli and Tuandai’s Year-End Battle*  
  https://tech.ifeng.com/a/20160110/41537083_0.shtml

These descriptions are **public statements by Yooli or related parties**. Public corporate-registration records do not always map cleanly onto the reported “acquisitions,” so the legal control method must be checked company by company.

For example, former Lixin CEO Ye Yanwei told media that Lixin’s registered majority shareholder Dai Hongguang was “a nominee holder for the Yooli side.” That remains a party statement and has not been confirmed by a judicial finding.

## 3. Puhui Kuaixin: agreements and court decisions show direct participation in consumer-loan origination

A public criminal judgment quoted a **2014-04-10 Strategic Cooperation Agreement** between Beijing Honghe Boji Financial Information Service Co., Ltd. and Shenzhen Puhui Kuaixin Financial Services Co., Ltd. The agreement allocated functions as follows.

### Puhui Kuaixin

- sourced consumer-finance borrowers;
- performed credit review;
- handled day-to-day management;
- provided borrowing information and documents to Yooli on the borrower’s authorization;
- assisted with the borrowing process.

### Honghe Boji / Yooli

- developed and managed registered lenders on the platform;
- provided information-intermediation services;
- matched loans;
- received borrower repayments and allocated them to lenders.

Source:

- *He XX, Zheng XX, Yin XX et al., First-Instance Criminal Judgment for Fraud* — (2018) Chuan 1403 Xing Chu No. 112  
  https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/何某1、郑某1、尹某1等诈骗罪一审刑事判决书

Later recovery cases repeatedly show a similar chain:

> Puhui Kuaixin sourced / reviewed the borrower → Yooli matched the loan with registered lenders → Chengxixiang or Guoyin Shengda provided a guarantee → borrower defaulted → Honghe Boji issued a compensation notice → guarantor compensated and obtained a recovery claim.

Related decision:

- *Chen Changhe v. Shenzhen Chengxixiang Financing Guarantee Co., Ltd., Second-Instance Judgment in Recovery-Right Dispute* — (2020) Qiong 96 Min Zhong No. 2868  
  https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/陈昌和、深圳市诚信祥融资担保有限公司追偿权纠纷民事二审民事判决书

These materials establish that Puhui Kuaixin performed borrower acquisition, underwriting, document submission, and related post-loan work rather than merely providing advertising leads.

## 4. Litong Auto Loans: public cases show origination, underwriting, collateral, servicing, and collection functions

Public court decisions concerning Beijing Litong Automobile Consulting Service Co., Ltd. and its branches show roles including:

- customer acquisition;
- borrower-document and credit review;
- financing-plan design;
- GPS installation;
- vehicle-mortgage processing;
- post-loan management and collection;
- in some transactions, guarantee or compensation obligations.

In some cases, after compensation Litong obtained recovery rights and priority rights in vehicle collateral.

Source:

- Guangzhou Intermediate People’s Court, (2023) Yue 01 Min Zhong No. 8824  
  https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/广东省广州市中级人民法院（2023）粤01民终8824号民事判决书

Historical recruitment materials described Litong as a “Yooli subsidiary.” That is useful evidence of organizational and brand affiliation, but cannot by itself substitute for corporate-registration or judicial evidence of a precise equity-control relationship.

## 5. Lixin Kuaijie and United Lixin Financial Leasing

Public reports stated that Yooli had completed acquisitions or integration of Lixin and other asset-side institutions. Former Lixin CEO Ye Yanwei also made the separate public claim that the registered majority shareholder acted as a nominee for Yooli.

Court materials additionally establish that:

- Lixin Kuaijie itself directly created or held loan claims in some transactions;
- Shenzhen Lixin Kuaijie participated in establishing Shenzhen United Lixin Financial Leasing Co., Ltd.;
- United Lixin later continued to deal with historical financial-leasing claims for years.

Beijing Financial Court’s **(2022) Jing 74 Min Zhong No. 1780** and related materials confirm the establishment and business relationships of United Lixin.

These materials show that the Lixin-related system did more than provide borrower lists to a P2P platform. It had the capacity to originate, hold, and later dispose of credit and financial-leasing assets in its own name.

## 6. Honghe Boji directly invested in asset-related companies

Public corporate relationships show that Beijing Honghe Boji Financial Information Service Co., Ltd. directly invested historically in several factoring, asset-management, and related businesses, including:

- Shenzhen Yooli Puhui Commercial Factoring Co., Ltd.;
- Shenzhen Honghe Boji Commercial Factoring Co., Ltd.;
- Shenzhen Yooli Puhui Asset Management Co., Ltd.;
- Beijing Fanglibao Asset Management Co., Ltd.;
- and other related entities.

Shenzhen Honghe Boji Commercial Factoring entered liquidation in **April 2020**.

The names and equity relationships of these companies do not establish how many Yooli underlying claims they held and do not establish the destination of lender funds. They do establish that Yooli’s operating entity directly formed or invested in businesses related to factoring and asset management.

## 7. Yooli also continued to use independent external asset channels

Deeper internal asset-side involvement did not mean that every underlying asset came from companies within the Yooli business system.

External cooperation channels now confirmed by court decisions, listed-company filings, or major asset-restructuring documents include:

### Tongsheng Microcredit

In 2014 it signed a cooperation agreement with Honghe Boji under which Tongsheng sourced, approved, and managed borrowers and provided joint and several guarantees.

### Hechuang Weilai / Youfenqi

Listed-company restructuring materials disclosed internet-finance cooperation involving Yooli, Hechuang Weilai, and Anjia Shixing, with an approved scale of **RMB 300 million**.

### Hemei Microcredit / Hemei Zhike

Listed-company disclosures show a cooperation agreement signed with Yooli in 2014 with a **RMB 500 million** cooperation scale. Hemei Group continued disclosing remaining Yooli-channel loan balances from 2018 through 2021.

## 8. What the current record directly confirms, and what it still does not

### Directly supported by current evidence

- Yooli management publicly described deeper asset-side expansion from around 2015;
- Yooli publicly said it had acquired or integrated Lixin Kuaijie, Puhui Kuaixin, and other asset-side institutions;
- Puhui Kuaixin actually performed borrower development, underwriting, and document submission for Yooli consumer loans;
- Litong actually performed borrower acquisition, underwriting, collateral, post-loan management, and collection in auto finance;
- Lixin and United Lixin had the capacity independently to originate, hold, and dispose of some credit / leasing assets;
- Honghe Boji’s operating entity directly invested in factoring, asset-management, and related companies;
- independent external channels such as Tongsheng, Hechuang Weilai / Youfenqi, and Hemei existed at the same time.

### Not directly established by current evidence

- the exact legal control method for every asset-side company described in media as “acquired”;
- the exact share of Yooli’s total historical origination attributable to each asset-side institution;
- whether each underlying loan came from a self-built / controlled channel or an independent external partner;
- whether undisclosed related-party transactions existed among the asset-side companies;
- whether lender funds were used to finance the platform, shareholders, actual controllers, or related parties themselves.

## 9. Analysis and judgment

Taken together, the evidence supports an **analytical conclusion** that Yooli’s later business model was not limited to passive information matching. The broader Yooli business system had become deeply involved in borrower acquisition, underwriting, asset servicing, collateral, post-loan management, guarantees, and collection. At the same time, independent external asset partners remained in use.

Accordingly, the most accurate description of the later structure is:

> **external asset partnerships coexisted with self-built, acquired, or otherwise controlled / deeply integrated asset-side operations.**

That is more consistent with the public record than either “all assets came from independent third parties” or “all assets were produced internally by Yooli.”

But this conclusion must **not** be extended into a conclusion of self-financing. Establishing self-financing would require transaction-level evidence of fund use, related-party relationships of borrower entities, bank-depository flows, and underlying-loan ledgers.

The 2018 special audit also stated that, within the scope and procedures it examined, it did not identify prohibited self-financing, fund pools, or similar conduct. That audit conclusion should likewise be limited to its period, sample, and audit procedures rather than treated as universal proof about all transactions or later periods.
