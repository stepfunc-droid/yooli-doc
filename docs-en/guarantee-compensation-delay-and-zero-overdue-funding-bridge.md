# Yooli Guarantee-Compensation Delays and the “Zero Overdue” Funding-Bridge Question

> Updated: 2026-10-16  
> Chinese original: [`有利网担保代偿延迟与零逾期资金桥疑点.md`](../docs/有利网担保代偿延迟与零逾期资金桥疑点.md)  
> This article continues the investigation into how Yooli could show very low or zero lender-side overdue during 2018–2019 while underlying defaults and cumulative compensation were already occurring. New court evidence shows that **at least in the Puhui Kuaixin–Chengxixiang and Puhui Kuaixin–Guoyin Shengda consumer-loan chains, formal guarantor compensation did not always occur immediately after the borrower's first missed payment; in some cases the delay was months or even years.**
>
> This means the earlier uniform explanation—“the borrower defaulted, the guarantor immediately compensated, therefore lender-side overdue remained normal”—is too simple. The more important question is: **between the borrower's first failure to pay according to contract and the guarantor's later formal compensation to Honghe Boji, who paid the lender's scheduled principal and interest, and through what mechanism?**
>
> **Evidentiary boundary:** these timing gaps do not establish that Yooli itself advanced money, operated a fund pool, or used new lenders' funds to pay old lenders. Public decisions establish the gap between borrower default and formal guarantor compensation; the intervening cash path requires bank-depository records, loan-level lender repayment records, and partner guarantee-deposit ledgers.
>
> This English version is a research translation. If wording differs, the Chinese original controls.

## 1. The strongest judgment: the court expressly found that Chengxixiang “did not make timely payment on the borrower's behalf”

The published first-instance judgment in *Shenzhen Chengxixiang Financing Guarantee Co., Ltd. v. Xie [redacted]* states the timing clearly.

Basic facts:

- loan originated: **2016-07-10**;
- first scheduled repayment: **2016-08-10**;
- 18 repayment installments;
- the borrower later failed to repay according to contract;
- the Yooli Loan Agreement required Chengxixiang, when the borrower failed to pay on time, to “immediately assume joint and several guarantee liability” and transfer the scheduled principal and interest into Honghe Boji's bank-supervised account when payment was not received on the due date;
- Honghe Boji did not issue its Compensation Notice until **2018-05-08**;
- Chengxixiang formally compensated on **2018-05-11**.

When examining penalty interest and liquidated damages, the court expressly said in substance:

> the plaintiff, as guarantor, did not repay promptly when the defendant failed to perform; it paid only after Yooli issued a compensation notice.

The court therefore did not pass all penalty interest and liquidated damages generated by that delay on to the borrower.

Source:

- Shenzhen Chengxixiang Financing Guarantee Co., Ltd. v. Xie, first-instance recovery-right judgment  
  https://www.chinalawyer.co/blog/深圳市诚信祥融资担保有限公司与谢思易追偿权纠纷一审民事判决书/

The importance is not merely that one payment was late. The decision directly disproves an overly simplified assumption: **although the contract required immediate compensation, the actual business did not necessarily operate that way.**

## 3. Formal compensation dates cluster noticeably in April–May 2018

Continuing to review Chengxixiang recovery cases produces repeated formal compensation dates:

- **2018-04-20**;
- **2018-04-27**;
- **2018-05-08**;
- **2018-05-11**.

Examples include:

- Chen Dan: 2014 loan, compensated 2018-04-20;
- Cao Zhen: 2016 loan, compensated 2018-05-08;
- Liu: 2015 loan, compensated 2018-05-08;
- Zhang: 2015 loan, compensated 2018-05-08;
- Xie: 2016 loan, compensated 2018-05-11.

In appellate materials in other litigation, Chengxixiang also asserted that nearly “ten thousand” similar effective cases existed nationwide in 2018. That was Chengxixiang's own litigation statement, not a court-verified total, but it does indicate large-scale judicial recovery activity.

The date clustering suggests a hypothesis worth testing: **some historical bad loans may not have produced formal guarantee-compensation documentation claim by claim at the time of first delinquency; instead, formal compensation records, claim transfers, and recovery actions may have been created in concentrated later windows.**

Without a full compensation ledger, this article records only the clustering and does not infer an internal batch-processing rule.

## 4. This makes Yooli's 2019 “historical overdue = zero” figure more difficult to explain

Yooli's 2019-10-31 operating page simultaneously disclosed:

- outstanding loan balance: about **RMB 12.6 billion**;
- current overdue projects: **0**;
- current overdue amount: **RMB 0**;
- **historical project overdue amount: RMB 0**;
- cumulative compensation: **RMB 602.75 million**;
- cumulative compensated cases: **86,441**.

Archive:

- https://wayback.yooli-action.online/statistic/platform/index.html

Under the National Internet Finance Association's *Internet Finance Information Disclosure — Individual Online Lending* standard:

- “historical project overdue amount” is the **cumulative principal of projects that had become overdue at any time up to the reporting date**;
- cumulative overdue compensation is the **sum compensated from a source other than the first repayment source**.

Standard:

- https://picpolicy.mofcom.gov.cn/file/20190402/76991554175004177.pdf

This creates a more specific reconciliation question.

Suppose a loan followed this timeline:

```text
Borrower stops monthly payments in 2016
        ↓
Guarantor formally compensates only in 2018
```

If the lender also failed to receive the scheduled principal and interest during the interval, one would normally expect historical overdue to arise. Yet Yooli's 2019 page still showed historical overdue amount as zero.

Conversely, if the lender **continued receiving money on time throughout the interval**, some other funding bridge must have operated before formal guarantee compensation.

The question is therefore no longer merely “did the guarantor compensate?” It becomes:

> **During the period when the borrower had already stopped paying and the guarantor had not yet formally compensated, where did the lender's cash come from?**

## 5. Public contracts prove that Yooli's historical system did contain bridging mechanisms beyond formal guarantee compensation

### 5.1 Tongsheng Microcredit agreement: a cooperation deposit could be debited directly

A 2014 cooperation agreement between Honghe Boji and Chongqing Tongsheng Microcredit, quoted in court decisions, provided that:

- Tongsheng should compensate after borrower delinquency;
- if Tongsheng failed to make a timely advance, **Honghe Boji could directly deduct the principal and interest currently due to lenders, together with related platform fees, from the cooperation deposit**;
- after Tongsheng later formally compensated, the claim would transfer automatically to Tongsheng for recovery.

Source:

- Chongqing No. 5 Intermediate People's Court, (2016) Yu 05 Min Zhong No. 3320  
  https://cn.govopendata.com/caipanwenshu/2016/07/15/63a5f7de-e415-4d9f-b35c-7874c7f718fc/

This proves that at least one real Yooli asset channel could use a sequence more complicated than simply:

```text
Borrower → guarantor → lender
```

It could instead be:

```text
Borrower normal repayment
       ↓ fails
Partner immediate advance / compensation
       ↓ not timely
Cooperation deposit debited first for lender payment
       ↓
Later formal compensation, claim transfer, and recovery
```

**This mechanism is established for the Tongsheng contract only; it cannot automatically be applied to Chengxixiang or Guoyin Shengda.**

### 5.2 Historical Dingcunbao agreements also listed “backup loans” among principal-and-interest protection mechanisms

Historical Dingcunbao agreements defined guarantee/protection methods to include:

- guarantees, mortgages, and pledges;
- compensation;
- claim repurchase;
- **extension of backup loans**, among other methods.

Sources:

- https://www.yooli.com/static/laws/dcbxyStatic.jsp
- https://www.yooli.com/static/laws/dcbxyStaticV2.jsp

The later V3 agreement continued listing “compensation, acquisition of claims, or extension of backup loans” among partner mechanisms supporting principal-and-interest repayment:

- https://www.yooli.com/static/laws/dcbxyStaticV3.jsp

This again shows that the lender-facing “paid on time” experience could be produced through multiple legal/cash mechanisms and cannot be read backward as proof that borrowers always paid normally.

### 5.3 Evidence for a platform-owned risk reserve remains insufficient

Old public media contain two types of statements:

- a 2015 People's Daily / China Securities discussion of rigid repayment expectations listed Yooli among P2P platforms historically described as maintaining risk reserves;
- a 2017 third-party review said Yooli's website did not disclose a risk reserve, while customer service allegedly said that a RMB 100 million reserve existed.

These are media/customer-service reports. No matching dedicated bank-account balance, formal reserve policy, or monthly contribution disclosure has been located. Therefore **a “RMB 100 million Yooli-owned risk reserve” cannot be treated as verified fact and cannot be used to explain the entire funding bridge.**

## 6. Yooli's own risk warning acknowledged that guarantors might be unable to compensate on time

Yooli's surviving *Online Lending Risk and Prohibited Conduct Notice* lists “risk that a guarantee company is unable to compensate” and states that, after a borrower fails to repay according to contract, unforeseeable factors may make a financing-guarantee company **unable to perform compensation obligations in a timely manner**, leaving lenders to bear the resulting risk.

Source:

- https://www.yooli.com/queryAgreeContentForRiskStatic.action?riskType=0

Thus delayed guarantee compensation was not a scenario wholly absent from Yooli's legal documentation.

But that still leaves a factual question unanswered: **where formal compensation was delayed for months or years, did the affected lenders experience the same delay in receiving cash?**

## 7. The most reasonable mechanism map now contains at least four potential sources of cash

Based on confirmed contracts and court materials, a historical scheduled lender payment could have come from:

```text
1. Normal borrower repayment

2. Immediate advance or formal compensation by guarantor / asset partner

3. Cooperation deposits, claim repurchase, backup loans, or other contractually pre-arranged credit-enhancement funds

4. Claim transfer — another lender acquires the old claim, allowing the original lender to exit in cash
```

Sources 1, 2, and 4 are broadly documented within the Yooli materials. Source 3 is proven to exist in the Tongsheng agreement and Dingcunbao contractual framework, but current evidence does not show which specific mechanism was used for individual Chengxixiang / Guoyin Shengda loans.

**These four sources must remain analytically separate.**

A guarantor formally paying in 2018 on a loan whose borrower defaulted in 2016 does not mean the lender necessarily waited until 2018 to receive money. Nor does it mean that any intervening 2016–2018 payments necessarily came from Yooli itself.

## 8. This changes how “the platform suddenly failed in 2020” should be described

A simpler earlier model was:

> borrower defaults → guarantor compensates → guarantor can no longer support the system → risk appears in 2020.

The model should now be upgraded:

> **After underlying default, the Yooli ecosystem could maintain normal lender-facing cash flows through different mechanisms: immediate partner advances, cooperation deposits, claim repurchase / backup loans, and claim transfer. Formal guarantee compensation and the resulting recovery right could be created well after the underlying first default. In 2019–2020, when demand from new assignees declined, underlying recovery worsened, and guarantee / asset-side institutions themselves came under pressure, several buffers contracted at once and historical credit risk increasingly passed directly to lenders.**

This fits the timing evidence better than either “COVID suddenly created tens of billions of bad loans” or “all bad loans had always been immediately covered by guarantors.”

---

## 9. The remaining evidence problem is transaction-level cash flow, not more webpage searching

More public cases may enlarge the sample, but the marginal value for the core question is now low. The evidence that could resolve the issue is:

1. **loan-level bank-depository flows** showing when each borrower actually paid each installment;
2. **lender repayment flows** showing when the lender actually received principal and interest for the same installment;
3. **guarantee / cooperation-deposit flows** identifying who filled any interim gap;
4. a complete ledger of **Compensation Notices** showing when Honghe Boji formally determined and requested compensation;
5. guarantor bank records for formal compensation;
6. **claim-transfer records** showing whether the same underlying loan was transferred to other lenders during the default period;
7. **extension / restructuring records** showing whether contractual changes altered how the original maturity or delinquency was treated in the system.

Until those materials appear, the most accurate conclusion is:

> **Public judicial materials prove that substantial gaps could exist between an underlying borrower's first default and the guarantor's formal compensation. Yooli's historical low/zero lender-side overdue therefore cannot be explained solely by immediate formal guarantee compensation. An intervening funding bridge is objectively a question that must be investigated, but public evidence is not yet sufficient to identify its specific source.**
