# Post-2020 Risk Exposure: Disclosure-Page Lag and Material-Event Questions

> Updated: 2026-10-16  
> Chinese original: [`2020年风险暴露后的信息披露页面滞后与重大事项疑点.md`](../docs/2020年风险暴露后的信息披露页面滞后与重大事项疑点.md)  
> This article examines only what Yooli's public website information-disclosure section showed **after risk had become visibly severe in 2020**. It does not by itself conclude that Yooli violated regulatory disclosure rules, because the platform may have disclosed some matters through its app, customer-service groups, regulatory submissions, or offline communications. The narrower question is whether the website's dedicated “Material Events” and “Audit Information” pages matched the contemporaneous risk situation.
>
> This English version is a research translation. If wording differs, the Chinese original controls.

## 1. The most visible finding: as late as March 2021, all seven categories on the “Material Events” page still said “None”

The restored Yooli “Material Events” page contained seven categories:

1. capital reduction, merger, division, dissolution, or bankruptcy application by the company;
2. the company formally entering bankruptcy proceedings;
3. the company being ordered to suspend business, rectify, or close;
4. major litigation or arbitration involving the company, investigation by competent authorities for suspected violations, or criminal / major administrative punishment;
5. major litigation, arbitration, investigation, punishment, or compulsory measures involving the legal representative, actual controller, principal officers, directors, supervisors, or senior management;
6. suspension of the company's main or all business;
7. fraud, conduct damaging lender interests, or other major events affecting the online-lending information intermediary's operations.

In the restored page, all seven categories continued to read: **“None.”**

Archived page:

- https://wayback.yooli-action.online/static/majorIssues/

Project-preserved Archive.org records show successful snapshots at least on:

- 2019-07-16;
- 2019-09-15;
- 2019-11-22;
- **2020-09-26**;
- **2020-12-05**;
- **2021-03-03**.

The substantive seven-category section remained unchanged in these versions; most visible changes were in shared header/footer elements.

Thus one website-level fact can be established:

> **After Yooli had publicly disclosed roughly RMB 2.678 billion of overdue loans, about RMB 4.891 billion of cumulative compensation, and long claim-transfer queues in July 2020, the dedicated “Material Events” page still contained no new event disclosure in archived versions from September 2020, December 2020, and March 2021.**

---

## 2. But RMB 2.678 billion of overdue cannot mechanically be equated with one of the seven material-event categories

This point requires caution.

Article 10 of the *Information Disclosure Guidelines for Online Lending Information Intermediaries* required disclosure within **48 hours** of the cause, current status, potential effect, and response measures for seven categories of material event. Those categories largely correspond to the seven fields on Yooli's page.

Official text:

- Jiangsu financial authority republication of the CBRC guidance  
  https://www.jsjrb.gov.cn/art/2017/8/30/art_34_25406.html

But a sharp rise in platform overdue does not automatically prove that one of those categories was legally triggered:

- capital reduction or judicial auction at an **asset partner** is not the same as Yooli itself reducing capital or entering bankruptcy;
- Chengxixiang cases being transferred to police does not establish that **Yooli itself** had been investigated;
- stopping **new loan origination** does not necessarily mean the company's “main or all business” had stopped, because Yooli continued managing existing claims, transfers, collections, and wind-down operations;
- large claim-transfer queues do not automatically map to a particular listed category.

Therefore the fact that the page remained “None” **does not, by itself, establish a legal violation of Article 10.**

The more useful question is when Yooli reached a regulatory definition such as “major operating risk” or “main business suspension,” and how regulators classified the situation at the time.

---

## 3. The Interim Measures separately required immediate reporting of major operating risk to regulators

Article 36 of the *Interim Measures for the Administration of the Business Activities of Online Lending Information Intermediaries* required a platform to take emergency measures and immediately report to the local financial regulator at its registration location after specified major events, including:

- major operating risk arising from poor management or other causes;
- major unlawful or noncompliant conduct by the platform or its directors, supervisors, and senior management;
- litigation arising from commercial fraud, including unlawful guarantees, exaggerated publicity, fabrication or concealment of facts, false information, false contracts, or improper handling of funds.

Articles 31 and 32 also required:

- timely disclosure of operational and management information in a prominent place on the website;
- periodic annual reports;
- periodic audits of key business processes and compliance/security assessments, with results disclosed;
- directors, supervisors, and senior management to ensure disclosures were true, accurate, complete, timely, and fair, without material omissions.

Official text:

- Cyberspace Administration of China  
  https://www.cac.gov.cn/2016-08/25/c_1119451974.htm

For Yooli, the question therefore has two layers:

1. **regulatory reporting:** after major risk developed in 2020, when and what did Yooli report to Beijing / Haidian financial regulators?
2. **public disclosure:** how much of the risk information known to regulators was publicly communicated, and when, in a form ordinary lenders could see and understand?

The public website can answer only part of the second question, not the first.

The project preserved six successful snapshots with distinct page metadata/content summaries:

- 2019-09-15;

The page continued to show three fixed categories:

- financial audit report;
- special audit report on key processes;
- compliance review / assessment report.

Reports that can currently be restored include:

- 2018 financial audit report;
- 2018 special audit of key processes;
- 2018 compliance assessment;
- earlier 2017 financial audit report.

Accordingly:

> **At least in the surviving public website archives, no new equivalent 2019 or 2020 audit/compliance report entry is visible even as late as March 2021.**

Archive documentation:

- https://github.com/stepfunc-droid/yooli-action/blob/master/wayback/archive/audit/README.md

This does not prove that no additional audit material was ever submitted to regulators. Public website disclosure and regulatory submission are separate acts. But for an ordinary lender relying on publicly accessible information, the external audit materials remained anchored to the pre-crisis period.

## 5. The 2018 special audit and compliance reports had limited scope

The existing 2018 reports are valuable, but they cannot be treated as a permanent attestation of all later asset authenticity and fund flows.

### 5.1 2018 special audit

The special audit reviewed fund depository, information disclosure, information technology, business compliance, and other key areas using sampling, interviews, analytical procedures, and similar methods.

In its business-compliance section, the report stated that within its procedures it had not identified a platform fund pool, platform advances for clients, self-financing or disguised self-financing, or operation as a credit intermediary. It also made clear that the report was based on materials available through the audit date.

That establishes what the auditor did **not identify within the scope and period of its procedures**. It is not equivalent to a forensic, transaction-by-transaction, full-cycle examination of all fund flows.

### 5.2 2018 compliance assessment

The lawyers' compliance assessment likewise reviewed contracts, policies, system materials, and public information supplied by the platform. It did not express a conclusion on every business and cash-flow development that later occurred in 2019–2020.

Accordingly, after material risk emerged in 2020, the explanatory value of continuing to show only 2018 reports naturally became weaker for users trying to assess current conditions.

---

## 6. At the same time, the website continued to retain strong “steady operation / risk control” messaging

The project's restored 2020 “Compliance Operation / Security” page continued to include statements such as:

- “more than 2,000 consecutive days of steady operation”;
- Huaxia Bank fund depository;
- small and diversified assets;
- FICO risk control;
- “mature risk-control process”;
- strict pre-loan and post-loan management;
- wording about ensuring or promoting repayment according to contract.

Some operating numbers on the page were current through **2020-07-31**.

At the level of website information architecture, lenders could therefore encounter all of the following after risk had already become public:

```text
Operations / security page: depository, risk control, steady-operation messaging
                │
                ├── Material Events page: all seven categories still “None”
                │
                └── Audit Information page: still mainly 2018 materials
```

That does not automatically make the safety statements legally false. But from the perspective of **effective risk communication**, there was an evident mismatch in emphasis.

A legitimate further question is whether an ordinary website user could quickly and centrally see the most important changes: long-term claim-transfer congestion, billions of yuan of overdue loans, rapidly increasing cumulative compensation, and the cessation of new loan origination.

---

## 7. The useful issue is not one stale sentence, but the disclosure time gap

For complaints, litigation, or regulatory submissions, the evidence should not be overstated as:

> “The Material Events page said ‘None,’ therefore Yooli concealed everything.”

A stronger and more defensible formulation is:

> **By mid-2020 Yooli's public operating data already showed dramatic changes in overdue amount, compensation, and claim-transfer conditions, and the platform publicly said it had stopped new loan origination on 2020-06-30. Yet surviving website snapshots show that its dedicated Material Events page still listed all seven categories as ‘None’ in September 2020, December 2020, and March 2021, while the Audit Information page continued to provide mainly 2018 materials. Whether the platform disclosed these risks fully and timely to all lenders through other channels requires further investigation.**

That formulation preserves the evidentiary force without exceeding what the archived pages prove.

---

## 8. The next high-value evidence is on the regulator side, not more copies of the same webpages

The public website evidence is now close to its limit. To assess whether disclosure was compliant, the missing materials are:

1. Beijing / Haidian financial-regulator inspections, interviews, risk warnings, and rectification documents concerning Yooli from 2019–2021;
2. the timing and content of any major-risk report Yooli submitted under Article 36 of the Interim Measures;
3. monthly underlying-loan overdue, compensation, and balance data reported to regulators and the National Internet Finance Association;
4. contemporaneous app notices, internal messages, SMS, WeChat/QQ groups, and other communications sent to all lenders;
5. whether 2019 and 2020 annual or special audits were actually completed and, if so, whether they were submitted only to regulators rather than publicly disclosed;
6. whether the 2020 cessation of new loan origination resulted from a regulatory requirement, including the date and wording of any such order.

Without those materials, continuing to search the same public website pages is likely to yield diminishing returns.
