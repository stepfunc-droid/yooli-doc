# Yooli Debt-Disposal Authorization and the Third-Party AMC Mechanism

> Updated: 2026-10-16  
> Chinese original: [`有利网债权处置授权与第三方AMC机制.md`](../docs/有利网债权处置授权与第三方AMC机制.md)  
> This article addresses a question that previously had not been separated clearly: **when a borrower defaulted and the guarantor also failed to compensate, under what contractual mechanism could Yooli dispose of lenders' claims, and how did the “third-party asset-management company / AMC” that appeared in 2021–2022 relate to those historical contracts?**
>
> **Evidentiary boundary:** public pages confirm that Yooli's standard agreements contained broad claim-disposal authorizations, and public notices confirm that a third-party asset-management company actually participated in cash purchases and claim substitutions during 2021–2022. The company's full legal name, shareholders, complete cooperation agreement, and pricing mechanism have not been located. It therefore cannot be equated with Zhangduoduo or any other known company, and the mechanism by itself does not prove a related-party transaction or below-market asset transfer.
>
> **Terminology:** for convenience this article uses “AMC” as shorthand for the third-party asset-management company or claim assignee described in Yooli agreements and wind-down notices. **It does not mean that the counterparty has been established as a licensed national financial AMC or local AMC.**
>
> This English version is a research translation. If wording differs, the Chinese original controls.

## 1. Contractual roles: the “Party E” authorized to dispose of claims was Yooli's operating entity

Yooli's publicly available standard *Loan Agreement* identifies five parties:

- Party A: borrower;
- Party B: registered platform lender(s);
- Party C: asset-side / service institution;
- Party D: guarantor;
- **Party E: Beijing Honghe Boji Financial Information Service Co., Ltd., the Yooli operating entity.**

The agreement further states that borrowers are screened and referred by Party C, register on Yooli operated by Party E, and are recommended to lenders through the platform.

Source:

- Yooli *Loan Agreement*  
  https://m.yooli.com/views/laws/contractAndLoanProtocol.jsp

This five-party structure is also confirmed in historical court decisions. In the Chen Changhe case, Puhui Kuaixin was Party C, Chengxixiang was Party D, and Beijing Honghe Boji was Party E. In the Zeng Xiaohua auto-loan case, Litong was Party C, Guoyin Shengda Party D, and Beijing Honghe Boji again Party E.

Sources:

- Chen Changhe / Shenzhen Chengxixiang recovery dispute  
  [《陈昌和、深圳市诚信祥融资担保有限公司追偿权纠纷民事二审民事判决书》（2020）琼96民终2868号](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/陈昌和、深圳市诚信祥融资担保有限公司追偿权纠纷民事二审民事判决书)
- Guangzhou Intermediate People's Court, (2023) Yue 01 Min Zhong No. 8824  
  [《广东省广州市中级人民法院（2023）粤01民终8824号民事判决书》](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/广东省广州市中级人民法院（2023）粤01民终8824号民事判决书)

Thus, when the contract says Party E may dispose of claims on behalf of lenders, the relevant entity is **Yooli's operating company acting under lender authorization.**

## 2. Clause 14.2 of the standard Loan Agreement: if both borrower and guarantor fail, Yooli may transfer the claim to a third-party AMC

Clause 14.2 of Yooli's currently accessible standard *Loan Agreement* provides that, if the borrower fails to repay in full and on time and the guarantor also fails to perform its guarantee obligations in full and on time, the lender irrevocably authorizes Party E to dispose of the claim on the lender's behalf.

Permitted methods include:

1. litigation and recovery against the borrower and guarantor;
2. engagement of third-party professional collection institutions;
3. **assignment of the claim to third parties, including asset-management companies and natural or legal persons capable of acquiring the claim;**
4. other methods that Party E considers most consistent with maximizing lender interests.

The agreement also allows Party E to advance disposal costs and deduct them from the proceeds of claim disposal.

Clause 14.3 provides broader agency powers, including signing claim-assignment and mandate agreements, participating in litigation and settlement, receiving litigation proceeds, and subdelegating authority.

Source:

- Yooli *Loan Agreement*  
  https://m.yooli.com/views/laws/contractAndLoanProtocol.jsp

An important limitation is that the currently available standard text does not display a specific execution year. **It establishes that this standard authorization exists, but it does not by itself establish that every historical lender and every older loan was governed by the identical version.**

## 3. Clauses 6.5–6.7 of the Investment Advisory and Management Service Agreement: the authorization also covers unmatured claims and gradual reduction of P2P business

Yooli's *Investment Advisory and Management Service Agreement (Lender)* grants broader powers.

Party A is expressly **Beijing Honghe Boji Financial Information Service Co., Ltd.**, and Party B is the lender.

### Clause 6.5: major risk or platform strategic transition

If the borrower or guarantor encounters major risk, if an offline institution is willing to acquire the lender's unmatured claims, or even if the **platform itself plans a strategic transformation and gradual reduction of P2P business**, the lender authorizes Yooli to:

- declare a loan due early;
- transfer claims;
- substitute claims;
- add credit enhancement;
- sign corresponding documents on the lender's behalf.

### Clause 6.6: borrower and guarantor both fail to perform

Yooli may:

- pursue litigation;
- engage third-party collection;
- **sell the claim to third-party asset-management companies, collection institutions, other institutions, or individuals;**
- deduct disposal costs from the proceeds.

### Clause 6.7: broad agency authority

This includes signing assignment/mandate agreements, initiating litigation, modifying or waiving claims, settling, receiving litigation proceeds, and subdelegating authority.

Sources:

- Yooli *Investment Advisory and Management Service Agreement (Lender)*  
  https://www.yooli.com/static/laws/tzzx.jsp
- PDF version  
  https://www.yooli.com/static/laws/word/tzzxyglfwxycjr.pdf

This means that using a third-party asset manager to handle problem claims was not necessarily an idea invented only during the 2021 wind-down. **At least in the standard contractual framework preserved by Yooli, claim disposal and acquisition by asset-management companies were already contemplated.**

The historical-version issue remains: public archived material has not yet established exactly when each version of these clauses first became effective.

## 4. Dingcunbao and Wuyoubao product agreements later incorporated similar disposal powers

The currently available *Dingcunbao Lending Consultation and Service Agreement* allows Yooli under clause 8.2 to act for lenders by:

- pursuing litigation;
- engaging third-party collection;
- transferring existing claims to asset-management companies and other third parties;
- requiring acceleration or confirming extensions.

Comparable provisions also appear in the Wuyoubao agreement.

Sources:

- Dingcunbao agreement  
  https://m.yooli.com/contract/dcbtzxy.do?typeId=-1
- Wuyoubao agreement  
  https://www.yooli.com/queryCurrentProductAgreeContentStatic.action?pageType=0

Thus Yooli's authority to handle problem claims appears at three levels: **loan agreement → lender service agreement → product agreement.**

## 5. This authorization sits after the earlier guarantee-compensation mechanism

Historical decisions show that the ordinary path had been:

```text
Borrower defaults
    ↓
Guarantor compensates under contract
    ↓
Lender receives principal/interest on time or eventually
    ↓
Claim / recovery right transfers to guarantor
    ↓
Guarantor pursues borrower
```

Puhui Kuaixin–Yooli–Guoyin Shengda / Chengxixiang consumer-loan cases repeatedly show Honghe Boji issuing a Compensation Notice, the guarantor paying, and the guarantor then obtaining a recovery right.

Clauses 14.2 and 6.6 address the next layer:

```text
Borrower does not pay
    ↓
Guarantor also does not compensate
    ↓
Lender still holds an unrealized claim
    ↓
Yooli uses delegated authority for collection, litigation, sale or substitution
    ↓
Third-party asset-management company becomes a possible assignee
```

That structure matches the post-2020 reality: as guarantee and compensation systems came under pressure, more claims entered a state in which neither borrower nor guarantor could produce full repayment and the platform shifted toward distressed-debt disposal.

## 6. 2021-04-01: Yooli formally exited P2P matching and announced the introduction of distressed-asset disposal institutions

On 2021-04-01, Yooli published *Recent Update on the Company* and formally announced its exit from online-lending matching.

The letter stated that:

- principal receivable at the start of risk resolution / stock reduction had been nearly **RMB 21 billion**;
- by end-March 2021, outstanding loan balance was about **RMB 8 billion**;
- fewer than **110,000 lenders** remained;
- underlying assets were mainly unsecured credit, consumer, and auto loans, with credit and consumer loans close to **90%**;
- the underlying overdue rate was close to **90%**;
- excluding roughly **RMB 5 billion of compensation already generated**, the platform described bad debts as close to **RMB 7 billion**.

Yooli announced that it would introduce **distressed-asset disposal institutions and service providers** to provide claim management and multiple exit routes:

1. pass-through of underlying-loan recoveries;
2. whole-account transfer;
3. shopping-mall route;
4. packages combining some cash and some mall value.

Source:

- Yooli public letter, 2021-04-01, Sohu republication  
  https://m.sohu.com/a/458468627_284577

At this point, the contractual idea of “third-party institutions disposing of problem claims” became an explicit wind-down mechanism.

## 7. 2021-12-28: the third-party asset-management company became central to the “final debt payout plan”

On 2021-12-28 Yooli published its *Final Debt Payout Plan*.

The notice said that, because of **funding problems at the third-party asset-management company**, existing cash and shopping-mall channels would gradually close, and Yooli would launch a “locality-based debt-claim recognition” scheme.

The structure was unusual:

```text
All claims in lender's original account
        ↓ transfer
Third-party asset-management company
        ↓ substitute at 1:1 of principal receivable
Locality-based claims selected by lender
        ↓
Lender holds / waits for original institution to collect / collects independently
        ↓ if still dissatisfied
May later sell at a discount to third-party asset-management company
```

The same third-party company therefore acted as:

- assignee of the original claims;
- intermediary in the claim pool;
- assignor of replacement claims;
- potential later discounted purchaser.

Sources:

- Jinronghu republication of Yooli notice  
  https://test.jinronghu.com/index.php/news/36102.html
- Kanong republication  
  https://daikuan-static.kanongyun.com/article-52616.html

This is the clearest public description of the third-party company's central role.

## 8. January 2022: Yooli confirmed that the cash-channel AMC had a formal cooperation agreement and that purchased claims had poor recoveries

On 2022-01-11, Yooli announced that the cash-transfer channel would close from 2022-02-01.

The notice disclosed several facts more explicitly than before:

- the **third-party asset-management company operating the cash-transfer channel had signed a cooperation agreement with Yooli**;
- that agreement was about to expire;
- the company had actually purchased Yooli-related claims;
- Yooli said the purchased claims had “quality below expectations and poor recoveries”;
- both sides therefore intended not to renew the cooperation.

Source:

- 2022-01-11 notice republication  
  https://daikuan-static.kanongyun.com/article-53139.html

This establishes that **the third-party AMC was not merely a virtual website matching role. It was an independent contractual counterparty that had a formal agreement with Yooli and actually deployed capital to acquire claims.**

But public notices still do not disclose:

- the AMC's full legal name;
- unified social credit code;
- shareholders and actual controller;
- whether it was related to Yooli, Wu Yiran, Ren Yong, or historical asset-side institutions;
- total face amount acquired;
- average purchase discount;
- actual recoveries after purchase;
- allocation of transaction gains or losses.

## 9. The central question is no longer whether Yooli had any authority to dispose of claims, but how disposal actually occurred

Given the standard contracts, it is inaccurate simply to say that “Yooli sold lender bad debts to an AMC without any authorization.” The agreements did provide broad agency powers.

The more important and still opaque questions are:

1. **Which claims were sold to the AMC?**
2. **At the time of transfer, had the guarantor already expressly refused or become unable to compensate?**
3. **Who was the AMC, and was it related to the platform, shareholders, executives, or historical asset-side institutions?**
4. **How was the transfer price determined—independent valuation, competitive process, or bilateral negotiation?**
5. **Where did the disposal consideration go: directly to lender accounts, a platform-administered account, or another entity?**
6. **How much collection, service, or disposal expense did the platform deduct?**
7. **How much did the AMC ultimately recover after purchase?**
8. **How did the economic quality of the 2021 locality-based “1:1 substitution” claims compare with the claims surrendered?**
9. **Why was the identity of the third-party asset manager never publicly identified during the wind-down?**

Any investigation of possible below-market asset transfer during the wind-down therefore needs to examine **identity, price, asset quality, cash flow, and related-party relationships together**, not just ask for the AMC's name.

## 10. Current conclusions

### Established by the public record

- Yooli's standard contracts expressly authorize the operating entity, when both borrower and guarantor fail to perform, to litigate, collect, or transfer claims to asset-management companies and other third parties on lenders' behalf.
- Yooli's standard lender-service agreement also permits claim transfer, substitution, and credit enhancement in circumstances of major risk, strategic transformation, or gradual reduction of P2P business.
- After April 2021, Yooli actually introduced distressed-asset disposal institutions into its wind-down plan.
- By late 2021, a third-party asset-management company was the central intermediary in the locality-based claim-recognition arrangement.
- In early 2022, Yooli explicitly said that the cash-channel AMC had a cooperation agreement with the platform, had actually bought claims, and had experienced poor recoveries.

### Not established by current evidence

- It cannot be confirmed that every historical lender agreement contained identical disposal authorization.
- The identity of the wind-down AMC remains unconfirmed.
- No evidence currently establishes that the AMC was related to Yooli shareholders, executives, or historical asset-side entities.
- The public record does not establish that transfers occurred below a reasonable market price.
- The public record does not establish that Yooli retained or diverted lender funds from AMC transactions.

The highest-value new evidence would be **any authentic AMC claim-assignment/substitution agreement, payment record, order detail, or the first/signature page of the cooperation agreement**. Such documents could finally identify the legal counterparty, transaction price, and cash-flow chain.
