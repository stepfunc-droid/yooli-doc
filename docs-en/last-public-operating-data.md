# Yooli's Last Public Operating Data

> Compiled: 2026-10-13  
> Chinese original: [`有利网最后公开运营数据整理.md`](../docs/有利网最后公开运营数据整理.md)  
> Purpose: reconstruct as far as possible Yooli's publicly disclosed lender counts, outstanding loan balance, overdue amount, compensation, and related metrics before and after it stopped new business and entered wind-down, while keeping different definitions used by the platform and media separate.
>
> This English version is a research translation. If wording differs, the Chinese original controls.

## 1. Conclusion first

The **latest verifiable Yooli public statement containing a relatively complete set of platform-wide stock figures** is the *Recent Update on the Company* published on 2021-04-01. It stated that, as of end-March 2021:

| Metric | Platform's public figure |
| --- | ---: |
| Current outstanding loan balance | **RMB 8.0 billion** |
| Remaining current lenders | **fewer than 110,000** |
| Principal receivable at start of risk resolution / stock reduction | **nearly RMB 21.0 billion** |
| Lenders at start of risk resolution / stock reduction | **more than 260,000** |
| Lenders with full recovery of principal and interest | **150,000** |
| Returns earned by those fully recovered lenders | **RMB 4.7 billion** |
| Cumulative loans facilitated | **RMB 74.5 billion** |
| Cumulative lenders served | **260,000** (platform's own wording) |
| Cumulative borrowers served | **5 million** |
| Compensation generated | **about RMB 5.0 billion** |
| Bad debt described by the platform | **nearly RMB 7.0 billion** |
| Underlying-project overdue rate described by the platform | **nearly 90%** |
| Share of underlying assets that were credit + consumer loans | **close to 90%** |

The same letter announced Yooli's exit from online-lending matching and proposed several resolution routes: pass-through of underlying recoveries, whole-account transfer, a shopping-mall route, and package arrangements.

Accordingly, the safest answer to “how many lenders and how much outstanding principal remained in Yooli's last complete public disclosure?” is:

> **As of end-March 2021, Yooli said its current outstanding loan balance was about RMB 8 billion and fewer than 110,000 lenders remained. It also said that when risk resolution and stock reduction began, principal receivable had been nearly RMB 21 billion and more than 260,000 lenders were involved.**

“Outstanding loan balance” generally corresponds to unpaid principal under borrower loan contracts. “Receivable” in lender-facing contexts can sometimes include expected interest. The two should not be treated as identical without explaining the source definition.

**After 2021, Yooli no longer published another equally complete combination of “remaining lender count + total outstanding loan balance” that has been located. It did, however, continue publishing serious-default borrower notices. Batches 1–295 contain readable overdue amounts totaling approximately RMB 9.794 billion by notice appearance. Because borrower identities are masked and no true unique claim ID exists, this number cannot be treated as RMB 9.794 billion of mutually exclusive bad-debt principal. A strict exact-fingerprint sensitivity analysis finds additional exact cross-batch appearances representing about 1.99% of cumulative amount; exact duplicates therefore exist but are not the main component of the aggregate.**

Another major conclusion is that **operating data cannot be interpreted in isolation from the contemporaneous disclosure and promotional environment.** In October 2019 Yooli had already disclosed roughly RMB 603 million in cumulative compensation, while lender-facing overdue projects and overdue amount were still shown as zero. During the same period the platform continued emphasizing compliance, regulatory connectivity, filing/pilot prospects, bank depository, risk control, and “safe and trustworthy” services. By mid-2020, overdue and compensation had deteriorated sharply and passed directly through to lenders.

## 2. October 2019: a critical “zero overdue + already substantial compensation” point

Yooli's archived operating-data page shows that as of **2019-10-31**:

| Metric | Website disclosure |
| --- | ---: |
| Outstanding loan balance | **about RMB 12.6 billion** |
| Current borrowers | **1,658,632** |
| Outstanding loan count | **2,664,969** |
| Interest balance | **about RMB 2.215 billion** |
| Cumulative compensation | **RMB 602.75 million** |
| Cumulative compensated cases | **86,441** |
| Overdue projects | **0** |
| Overdue amount | **RMB 0** |
| Project overdue rate | **0%** |

Archive:

https://wayback.yooli-action.online/statistic/platform/index.html

### Is “compensation exists, overdue is zero” internally contradictory?

Not necessarily.

Under the regulatory *Information Disclosure Guidelines for Online Lending Information Intermediaries*:

- “overdue amount” focuses on amounts of principal and interest that **the lender did not receive according to contract when due**;
- “cumulative compensation amount” is the total amount repaid by a third party because of borrower default or similar reasons.

If the underlying borrower defaulted but a guarantor or other third party paid the lender in time, underlying credit risk had occurred while lender-facing “overdue amount” could remain at zero.

Official rule:

https://www.jsjrb.gov.cn/art/2017/8/30/art_34_25406.html

Therefore Yooli's 2019 zero-overdue field should not simply be labeled fabricated.

But the combination raises a more important question:

> **By October 2019 public figures already showed that several hundred million yuan of underlying borrower default had required third-party compensation, while the most intuitive lender-facing overdue metric remained zero. Whether the platform adequately disclosed underlying default levels, compensating parties, compensation capacity, and compensation pressure so that lenders could understand the changing risk requires further investigation.**

### “Zero overdue” was also part of the long-running safety narrative

A surviving Yooli mobile help page says in substance:

> “Since Yooli went online, the platform has not had a single overdue case; lenders received the corresponding principal and return on the repayment date.”

The same page separately explains that, after a borrower becomes overdue, a guarantor completes compensation within a specified period.

Page:

https://m.yooli.com/views/appCommon/help/safety.jsp

This shows that “no overdue” in Yooli's promotional context was closer to **“lenders ultimately received their money on time”** than to “underlying borrowers never defaulted.” That distinction is central to understanding the later risk transmission.

## 3. The 2018–2019 operating data existed within a positive compliance and safety information environment

Looking only at declining outstanding balances could suggest that Yooli had clearly entered risk reduction from 2018 onward and lenders should have become cautious. But other public signals were not simply “the platform is preparing to exit.”

Current evidence establishes that:

- in 2017 Yooli was among the first pilot institutions in the National Internet Finance Association's information-disclosure system;
- later in 2017 it was also among the first institutions connected to product registration;
- at end-2018 CEO Wu Yiran publicly said Yooli had consistently pursued compliant development, lenders continued receiving contractual repayments through the industry shock, and compliant leading platforms would have better prospects in 2019;
- in January 2019 Yooli's “risk education” section posted an article titled *Chinese Academy of Social Sciences: P2P Will Still Have Broad Development Space After Filing Is Implemented*;
- in April 2019 the same section posted *Filing Countdown: P2P to Reopen in June; Beijing Administrative Verification to End*;
- after regulators introduced the principle “mature one, include one” for a regulatory pilot, Yooli's September 2019 promotional article described the policy as favorable to “online-lending platforms such as Yooli that have consistently adhered to compliant development”;
- the same article emphasized bank depository, registered capital of RMB 1 billion, real-time regulatory data reporting, and information disclosure, and said the platform would continue providing “safe, authentic and trustworthy” products and services.

Archives:

- https://wayback.yooli-action.online/education/detail/121.html
- https://wayback.yooli-action.online/education/detail/128.html
- https://wayback.yooli-action.online/media/detail/191.html
- https://wayback.yooli-action.online/static/recordInfo/index.html

A necessary counterpoint: Yooli's own archived “filing information” page still showed formal local P2P filing fields as “to be disclosed after filing.” The evidence therefore does **not** support saying that Yooli had formally completed regulatory P2P filing or had been officially named as a regulatory-pilot institution.

A more accurate description is:

> **Yooli participated for years in information disclosure, product registration, real-time data reporting, and other regulatory/self-regulatory infrastructure, while continuing to promote its compliance construction and positive expectations of entering a future regulatory pilot.**

See:

- [Yooli “Compliance / Pilot” Promotion and Risk Disclosure](./compliance-pilot-promotion-and-risk-disclosure.md)

## 4. June–July 2020: risk began passing directly through to lenders

In July 2020 multiple media outlets cited Yooli website or National Internet Finance Association data. The figures are broadly cross-consistent.

### 4.1 Main late-July 2020 figures

| Metric | Public late-July 2020 figure |
| --- | ---: |
| Outstanding loan balance | **RMB 11.418 billion** |
| Interest balance | **more than RMB 1.8 billion** |
| Outstanding loan count | **more than 1.98 million** |
| Current lenders | **160,400** |
| Cumulative compensation | **RMB 4.891 billion** |
| Overdue projects | **269,879** |
| Overdue amount | **RMB 2,678,274,500 (about RMB 2.678bn)** |
| Project overdue rate | **13.60%** |
| Historical project overdue rate | **2.95%** |
| Amount overdue rate (media citing platform definition) | **20.22%** |

A Jinronghu report dated these figures to **2020-07-27**.

Simply adding RMB 11.418 billion principal balance and “more than RMB 1.8 billion” interest balance produces a nominal principal-plus-expected-interest amount above **RMB 13.218 billion**. That is an arithmetic inference from two fields, not a platform-published “total receivable” field, and should be described accordingly.

### 4.2 From RMB 603 million in compensation to RMB 4.891 billion

Cumulative compensation rose from about **RMB 603 million** on 2019-10-31 to about **RMB 4.891 billion** by late July 2020, an increase of more than RMB 4.2 billion in less than a year.

At the same time, the previous system in which compensation could keep lender-facing overdue at zero no longer held: public overdue reached **RMB 2.678 billion**.

This timing is more informative than any single overdue-rate field. It suggests a risk path such as:

> underlying borrower defaults rise → third-party compensation absorbs losses first → lenders can still receive money / public overdue remains zero → compensation grows rapidly → compensation and claim-transfer systems can no longer absorb all stress → overdue appears directly to lenders.

### 4.3 Borrower count and overdue at end-June 2020

Media citing industry-association data said that at end-June:

- current borrowers: **1,379,331**;
- decline from May: only **5,556**;
- overdue amount: **RMB 2.678 billion**;
- project overdue rate: **13.60%**;
- amount overdue rate: **20.22%**.

Nanfang also reported that Yooli's website showed cumulative lending of about **RMB 74.517 billion** by 2020-06-30 and approximately **4.98 million borrowers** had completed borrowing.

Those numbers broadly connect with the April 2021 platform letter describing RMB 74.5 billion cumulative lending and 5 million borrowers served.

### 4.4 The contemporaneous safety page still contained strong reassurance

An archived Yooli “Compliance Operation / Security” page states that its data were current through **2020-07-31**, while still displaying phrases such as:

- “Yooli has operated steadily for more than 2,000 days”;
- “small and diversified, effectively reducing risk”;
- “careful selection, only high-quality borrowers”;
- “layered safeguards, ensuring repayment on time.”

Archive:

https://wayback.yooli-action.online/static/security/index.html

This may have been a static page retained from earlier periods rather than newly issued marketing in July 2020. It nevertheless matters when assessing whether the platform's overall public information allowed users to understand the severity of risk.

## 5. Why public pages also showed “RMB 104.875 billion transaction volume” and “33.39 million lenders”

A Securities Times report on 2020-07-25 cited Yooli's homepage as showing, on July 24:

- transaction volume: **RMB 104,874,909,628**, about **RMB 104.875 billion**;
- displayed “lender” count: **33,391,754**.

These figures cannot be mixed directly with regulatory-style “cumulative loans facilitated” or “current lender count.”

Yooli's website used different fields such as “total transaction volume,” “lenders joining Yooli,” and “cumulative lending amount.” The roughly RMB 104.875 billion homepage figure was a “transaction volume” metric, whereas the roughly RMB 74.517 billion figure used in regulatory/operating disclosure was a “cumulative loan amount” metric. The labels differ and should not be treated as identical.

Similarly, the 33,391,754 displayed figure differs by two orders of magnitude from contemporaneous **160,400 current lenders**, media reports of roughly 967,600 cumulative lenders, and Yooli's 2021 statement that it had provided investment services to 260,000 lenders. The de-duplication and counting rules are not known.

Accordingly:

> **33,391,754 should not be used as the number of actual remaining lenders or the total number of damaged lenders.**

For litigation, police reports, or media citation, metrics with clear regulatory meaning—such as current lenders, outstanding loan balance, and principal receivable—should be preferred.

## 6. Why the 2021-04-01 letter is especially important

*Recent Update on the Company* was not an ordinary monthly operating report. It was a broad stock-problem statement issued as Yooli formally exited P2P matching.

It stated:

- at the start of risk resolution and stock reduction, **principal receivable was nearly RMB 21 billion**;
- existing lenders then numbered **more than 260,000**;
- by end-March 2021, **150,000 lenders had recovered principal and interest in full**;
- outstanding loan balance had fallen to **RMB 8 billion**;
- remaining lenders were **fewer than 110,000**;
- unsecured credit and consumer loans represented close to 90% of underlying assets;
- Yooli said underlying-project overdue rate was nearly **90%** and bad debt nearly **RMB 7 billion**.

These figures are particularly useful because they present both the retrospective starting point and the March 2021 state.

The decline from nearly RMB 21 billion of principal receivable to RMB 8 billion of outstanding loan balance **cannot be interpreted simply as RMB 13 billion of cash paid by the platform to lenders**. It may include ordinary borrower repayment, compensation, claim transfers, discounted exits, and asset disposal. The platform letter did not provide a complete cash-flow bridge, so the balance difference alone cannot establish the amount of cash payout.

The letter also provides a striking retrospective comparison: in 2019 Yooli still emphasized zero overdue, compliance, regulatory-pilot benefits, and safety; in 2021 the platform itself described underlying overdue near 90% and bad debt near RMB 7 billion.

That does not prove that Yooli already knew the entire later loss amount in 2019. It does establish that **the lender-facing “zero overdue” figure in 2019 and the underlying risk level publicly described by Yooli in 2021 were dramatically different layers of information.**

## 7. After 2021: no later complete stock report, but serious-default notices continued

On 2021-12-28 Yooli published its “final debt payout plan” and introduced locality-based debt-claim recognition. In January 2022 it announced closure of the original cash-transfer channel and shifted work toward claim recognition.

Among the later notices located, **none provides a newer complete pair of “remaining lender count + total outstanding loan balance.”** Therefore the April 2021 figures—**RMB 8 billion and fewer than 110,000 lenders**—remain the latest verifiable complete stock figures located so far.

That does not mean quantitative information disappeared. Yooli continued publishing serious-default borrower lists. The archive currently covers **295 batches** from **2020-06-25 through 2024-10-29**.

| Metric | Batches 1–295 |
| --- | ---: |
| Official notice records | **621,387** |
| Cumulative readable overdue amount | **RMB 9,794,144,241.81** |
| Equivalent | **about RMB 9.794bn** |
| 2016–2018 originations | **about RMB 8.542bn, 87.2%** |
| 2017 originations | **about RMB 4.853bn, 49.6%** |

Archive and statistics:

https://github.com/stepfunc-droid/yooli-public-notice-archive

The **RMB 9.794 billion is a cumulative notice-appearance overdue amount, not the platform's live overdue balance on any single date**. Its safest use is to study the cumulative scale and vintage structure of publicized problem assets.

A further look-through based on loan start date plus original term finds that, excluding 2,000 batch-214 records with no loan-term data, **481,434 of 619,387 calculable records** had final contractual maturity by **2019-10-31**, corresponding to roughly **RMB 6.26570 billion** of later notice-appearance overdue amount. **301,479 records, about RMB 2.57891 billion**, had final contractual maturity by end-2018. After removing exact cross-batch strict-fingerprint duplicates, the pre-2019-10-31 maturity total remains **441,695 records, about RMB 6.11316 billion**.

That does not prove those loans were already classified as bad debt on 2019-10-31. It does establish that many loans later appearing in serious-default notices had already passed original final contractual maturity by then.

Analysis:

https://github.com/stepfunc-droid/yooli-public-notice-archive/blob/master/statistics/maturity-analysis.md

### “Nearly RMB 7bn bad debt” and the RMB 9.794bn notice total cannot be directly subtracted

Yooli's April 2021 statement of nearly **RMB 7 billion** bad debt and the later approximately **RMB 9.794 billion** cumulative serious-default notice amount differ superficially by about RMB 2.794 billion. That is not an interpretable “bad-debt discrepancy” without further work.

The figures differ in time and definition, while RMB 9.794 billion is a notice-appearance aggregate. Strict-fingerprint sensitivity analysis identifies exact additional cross-batch records totaling about **RMB 195.22 million**, roughly **1.99%** of calculable notice amount. Exact duplicates are not enough to explain the apparent gap, but the same claim with a changed remaining balance may not be caught by strict fingerprinting, and later deterioration plus different definitions also matter.

**The RMB 2.794 billion apparent difference must not be presented as proof of underreported bad debt.** The two figures should remain separate metrics.

## 9. Numbers that require particular caution

### 9.1 “33,391,754 lenders”

A Securities Times report did cite this homepage number, but it differs from regulatory current-lender figures by roughly two orders of magnitude. It should be preserved as a homepage cumulative-user/participation-style metric, not used as the actual number of lenders owed money.

### 9.2 “967,600 cumulative lenders”

Some 2020 media reports said cumulative lenders reached about 967,600, while Yooli's 2021 announcement used language equivalent to “provided investment services to 260,000 lenders.” The counting methods clearly differ. Current evidence does not identify the de-duplication rules, so both figures may be preserved with labels but should not be forced into a single metric.

### 9.3 “RMB 21bn principal receivable”

This was a retrospective 2021 statement describing the **start of risk resolution / stock reduction**, not the March 2021 current balance. By end-March 2021 current outstanding loan balance was already about RMB 8 billion.

### 9.4 “RMB 8bn outstanding loan balance” is not the same as “the platform company owes RMB 8bn”

The figure represents unpaid principal under underlying P2P borrower contracts. Yooli consistently positioned itself legally as an information intermediary. The figure therefore cannot, by itself, be treated as a liability on the platform company's corporate balance sheet. Any fund-pool, self-financing, retained-repayment, or similar allegation requires separate evidence.

### 9.5 “Zero overdue” does not mean zero borrower defaults

By October 2019 Yooli had about RMB 603 million of cumulative compensation while overdue amount remained zero. Under disclosure definitions, third-party timely compensation could prevent a borrower default from becoming lender-facing overdue. Overdue and compensation therefore must be read together when assessing underlying asset quality.

### 9.6 “Nearly RMB 7bn bad debt” and “RMB 9.794bn cumulative notice overdue” cannot be subtracted directly

The former is Yooli's bad-debt description as of March 2021; the latter is cumulative notice-appearance overdue from 2020–2024. Exact duplicate sensitivity accounts for about 1.99%, but the metrics still differ in date, definition, and unique-claim identification. The apparent difference should not be characterized as “underreported bad debt.”
