# Yooli's Early External Asset Partners and Guarantee Network

> Updated: 2026-10-16  
> Chinese original: [`有利网早期外部资产合作机构与担保网络.md`](../docs/有利网早期外部资产合作机构与担保网络.md)  
> This article fills in a previously underexamined layer of Yooli's asset-side history: **in addition to better-known institutions such as Lixin, Puhui Kuaixin, and Litong, Yooli obtained loan assets through multiple microloan companies, consumer-finance technology firms, and guarantee institutions, embedding borrower sourcing, guarantee compensation, and recovery into cooperation agreements.**
>
> Priority is given to listed-company disclosures, legal opinions issued for major asset restructurings, and effective court decisions because those sources often disclose cooperation limits, guarantors, fee structures, and outstanding balances that are not visible in ordinary corporate-registration pages.
>
> This English version is a research translation. If wording differs, the Chinese original controls.

## 1. Tongsheng Microcredit: by 2014 Yooli already used a complete model of asset origination + layered guarantees + automatic claim assignment

Multiple Chongqing court decisions reproduce the cooperation model between Yooli and **Chongqing Liangjiang New Area Tongsheng Microcredit Co., Ltd.**

### 1.1 The 2014-04-25 Crowdfunding Internet-Finance Project Cooperation Agreement

The agreement divided responsibilities clearly:

- **Honghe Boji / Yooli** developed and managed lenders, provided information services, and matched loans;
- **Tongsheng Microcredit** developed borrowers, reviewed applications, collected loans, and performed day-to-day management;
- Tongsheng assumed **joint and several guarantee liability** for borrower obligations under the loan agreement;
- the parties established a jointly supervised account used for borrower repayments, platform comprehensive service fees, and asset-side fees.

If a borrower became delinquent and Tongsheng failed to make a timely advance, Yooli could deduct the principal and interest due to lenders, together with relevant fees, from the cooperation deposit.

Source:

- Chongqing No. 5 Intermediate People's Court, (2016) Yu 05 Min Zhong No. 3320  
  https://cn.govopendata.com/caipanwenshu/2016/07/15/63a5f7de-e415-4d9f-b35c-7874c7f718fc/

### 1.2 After compensation, lenders' claims automatically transferred to Tongsheng without separate consideration

The agreement provided that once Tongsheng performed compensation, the lender's corresponding claim against the borrower would automatically and gratuitously transfer to Tongsheng. Tongsheng could then sue, further assign the claim, or otherwise recover it, while Yooli—acting as the lenders' authorized representative—would assist by issuing claim-assignment documentation.

This is highly consistent with the later Puhui Kuaixin–Chengxixiang / Guoyin Shengda consumer-loan structure. It shows that **“guarantor protects lender principal and interest first, then obtains the bad-debt recovery claim after compensation” was already a mature Yooli business mechanism at an early stage.**

### 1.3 Some projects used a first guarantor plus Tongsheng as a second-layer backstop

In the He Xu case:

- **Chongqing Jiqian Enterprise Management Co., Ltd.** provided borrower consulting and post-loan services;
- Jiqian first assumed joint and several guarantee liability;
- if Jiqian failed to perform fully, Yooli would notify Tongsheng in writing to perform its own joint guarantee;
- the borrower also provided third-party counter-security to Tongsheng.

The structure could therefore be:

```text
Borrower
  ↓
Asset-service company / first guarantor (Jiqian)
  ↓ fails to perform
Tongsheng Microcredit / second guarantor
  ↓ compensates
Lender claim transfers to Tongsheng
  ↓
Tongsheng recovers from borrower and counter-guarantor
```

### 1.4 Court decisions also show fees deducted directly from nominal principal

In (2016) Yu 0108 Min Chu No. 1162:

- contractual principal: RMB 500,000;
- Yooli deducted an upfront fee of RMB 6,250;
- borrower actually received RMB 493,750.

In another RMB 1 million loan case, Honghe Boji deducted RMB 5,000 before disbursing RMB 995,000.

Sources:

- Chongqing Nan'an District People's Court, (2016) Yu 0108 Min Chu No. 1162  
  https://cn.govopendata.com/caipanwenshu/2016/10/30/f7da5f30-0de5-4943-ba59-10e21149d0fc/
- Chongqing Nan'an District People's Court, (2016) Yu 0108 Min Chu No. 10829  
  https://cn.govopendata.com/caipanwenshu/2017/8/11/98fe16cf-2541-486f-9ae7-5f1ec7f87941/

These enterprise/business-loan fee ratios were far below the 20%–40% one-time management fees later seen in some consumer-installment samples, so fee structures across asset classes should not be conflated.

## 2. Hechuang Weilai / Youfenqi: listed-company restructuring materials disclose a RMB 300 million Yooli credit line

**Beijing Hechuang Weilai Network Technology Co., Ltd.** operated the “Youfenqi” business, which developed from student consumer installments into cash installments and small personal-finance services.

In 2016 Huasu Holdings proposed acquiring 51% of Hechuang Weilai. Because the transaction constituted a major listed-company asset restructuring, the legal opinion disclosed cooperation contracts between Hechuang and multiple funding platforms, allowing one historical Yooli asset channel to be quantified.

### 2.1 Yooli, Hechuang Weilai, and Anjia Shixing signed an internet-finance project cooperation agreement

The restructuring legal opinion identified:

- the contract among **Beijing Honghe Boji Financial Information Service Co., Ltd., Beijing Hechuang Weilai Network Technology Co., Ltd., and Beijing Anjia Shixing Financing Guarantee Co., Ltd.**, together with supplemental agreements;
- asset source: customers of Hechuang Weilai's platform;
- funding source: platform customers of Beijing Honghe Boji—i.e., Yooli lenders;
- guarantors: **Hechuang Weilai, Beijing Anjia Shixing Financing Guarantee Co., Ltd., Li Hui, and Fang Ping**;
- credit line: **RMB 300 million**;
- as of 2016-03-31, Hechuang customers still owed **RMB 11,480,733.80** in principal and interest through this channel.

Source:

- Grandway Law Offices, legal opinion on Huasu Holdings' cash purchase of assets  
  https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2016-05-23/1202334259.PDF

The same filing showed Hechuang supplying assets to Renren Jucai, Souyidai, and other funding platforms. Like Litong later, **Youfenqi functioned as an asset-production platform capable of connecting similar borrowers to multiple capital providers.**

### 2.2 Hechuang itself was a guarantor, and founders provided personal guarantees

The Yooli channel was not guaranteed only by an unrelated guarantee company. The disclosed documents show that Hechuang itself, Anjia Shixing, Li Hui, and Fang Ping jointly assumed guarantee obligations.

Thus some early Yooli cooperation structures used multiple layers of credit enhancement: **asset-side company + professional financing-guarantee company + founder personal guarantees.**

### 2.3 Beijing Anjia Shixing was a formally licensed financing-guarantee institution

Beijing regulatory disclosures show that:

- Beijing Anjia Shixing Financing Guarantee Co., Ltd. had institution code **Jing A000055**;
- it received a 2016 Beijing financing-guarantee composite rating of **A-bj**;
- in 2022 the Beijing local financial regulator announced continuation of its financing-guarantee business license with long-term validity.

Sources:

- Beijing financing-guarantee institution list  
  https://jrj.beijing.gov.cn/tztg/201805/t20180515_727486.html
- 2016 comprehensive rating  
  https://jrj.beijing.gov.cn/ztzl/xzxkcfsgs/xzxkjg/201911/t20191129_749678.html
- 2022 license-continuation notice  
  https://jrj.beijing.gov.cn/ztzl/xzzfgs/qtxzzfjg/202201/t20220120_2597125.html

The guarantor on this channel therefore was not merely an unlicensed shell company.

### 2.4 Hechuang planned to stop entering new “guaranteed loan contracts” after legacy duties ended

The restructuring legal opinion specifically recorded Hechuang's statement that after its existing contractual guarantee obligations had been fulfilled, it would terminate those guaranteed-loan arrangements and would not sign new guarantee-style loan contracts with those lending platforms.

This reflects how the asset side began changing the model in response to the 2016 regulatory environment.

## 3. Hemei Microcredit / Hemei Zhike: a RMB 500 million cooperation channel with continuously disclosed balances

Listed-company disclosures by Hemei Group reveal another Yooli asset channel in unusual detail.

### 3.1 Cooperation agreement signed 2014-12-31, with a RMB 500 million scale

In a response to the Shenzhen Stock Exchange, Hemei Group disclosed:

- primary creditor/funding channel: **Beijing Honghe Boji Financial Information Service Co., Ltd. (Yooli)**;
- compensating parties: **Hemei Microcredit and Shenzhen Hemei Zhihui Technology Co., Ltd. (Hemei Zhike)**;
- debtors: natural persons and legal persons;
- cooperation scale: **RMB 500,000,000**;
- agreement date: **2014-12-31**;
- guarantee period: from disbursement of loans under the cooperation agreement through two years after maturity.

The guarantee structure was:

1. Hemei Zhike and Hemei Microcredit jointly and severally guaranteed all borrower obligations;
2. Hemei Microcredit also guided and managed Hemei Zhike's business and guaranteed Hemei Zhike's obligations;
3. if Hemei Zhike failed to perform, lenders could directly demand performance from Hemei Microcredit.

Source:

- Hemei Group corrected response to Shenzhen Stock Exchange inquiry  
  https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2019-09-27/1206952595.PDF

### 3.2 The contract disclosed a specific fee model

The listed-company disclosure stated that:

- Hemei Zhike charged a one-time **2% handling fee**;
- it also charged an intermediary fee calculated as the balance between a “2.3% monthly comprehensive fee” and the annualized comprehensive funding cost;
- Yooli loan contracts separately allowed Hemei Microcredit to charge consulting, service, and collection fees.

Thus lender interest was only one component of the borrower's total financing cost; the asset side could receive additional upfront and intermediary income.

A recurring table field saying the “compensation obligation has been completed” **must not be read as saying that the roughly RMB 92 million of loans had all been repaid**, because the same disclosure continued to show a large outstanding loan balance. The field appears to describe the company's disclosed status of a guarantee/compensation obligation while the balance field tracks remaining loans. The exact relationship requires the original cooperation ledger.

### 3.4 Hemei Group itself entered severe liquidity distress in 2019–2020

Hemei's financial-services subsidiaries rapidly lost value and control:

- **51% of Hemei Zhike** was judicially auctioned because of the listed company's debt dispute for only **RMB 2 million**; corporate-registration transfer was completed on 2020-05-09 and delivery on 2020-05-31, after which it was no longer consolidated by Hemei Group;
- Hemei Group's 2020 annual report disclosed Hemei Zhike's net assets at disposal as approximately **negative RMB 397 million**;
- **51% of Hemei Microcredit** was also auctioned on 2020-12-04 for **RMB 2 million**, although later enforcement procedures produced cancellation/dispute issues;
- Hemei Group described Hemei Microcredit as a core financial subsidiary responsible for customer development, reviewing loan materials, managing disbursement and recovery, and bearing compensation obligations for multiple funding channels.

Sources:

- Hemei Zhike equity disposal / Hemei Group 2020 annual report  
  https://epaper.cs.com.cn/zgzqb/images/2021-04/30/B283/zqB28330.pdf
- Hemei Microcredit judicial auction / inquiry response  
  https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2021-01-05/doc-iiznezxt0585862.shtml

Thus by the time Yooli's repayment crisis became public in mid-2020, an asset/guarantee system that had once supported a RMB 500 million Yooli cooperation limit was itself under severe financial stress.

This does **not** prove that “Hemei's collapse caused Yooli's crisis.” It does provide another documented example supporting a narrower conclusion:

> **Yooli's historically low lender-side overdue figures depended in part on layered external asset-side and guarantee institutions; by 2019–2020, some of those partners were themselves losing financial capacity.**

## 4. Viewed together, early Yooli was not a simple borrower-to-platform structure

Three representative cooperation structures can now be reconstructed:

| Asset-side / partner | Main assets | Credit-enhancement structure | Known cooperation scale / balance |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Tongsheng Microcredit + Jiqian and others | Enterprise/business loans | First guarantor + Tongsheng second-layer guarantee + counter-security | Individual cases RMB 0.5m–1.08m; total limit not yet located |
| Hechuang Weilai / Youfenqi | Student installments and small personal finance | Hechuang + Anjia Shixing + personal guarantees from Li Hui / Fang Ping | Yooli credit line RMB 300m; March 2016 balance about RMB 11.48m |
| Hemei Microcredit / Hemei Zhike | Small loans to natural/legal persons | Joint and several guarantees by Hemei Zhike + Hemei Microcredit | Cooperation scale RMB 500m; remaining balance around RMB 92m during 2018–2021 |

Together with separately investigated institutions—Lixin Kuaijie, Puhui Kuaixin, Litong Auto, Chengxixiang, Guoyin Shengda, and United Lixin Financial Leasing—the actual asset and risk network was clearly more than a two-dimensional “platform + borrower” relationship:

```text
Lender funds
   ↓
Yooli platform
   ↓
Multiple asset-side / borrower-sourcing and review institutions
   ↓
Borrowers
   ↕
Guarantee companies / asset-side guarantees / founder personal guarantees / counter-security
   ↓ after default
Compensation → claim transfer → recovery / further assignment
```

This is important when interpreting Yooli's 2019 “zero overdue” reporting: **as long as external guarantee and compensation networks continued paying, deterioration in underlying borrowers did not necessarily appear immediately as lender-side overdue.**

## 5. The highest-yield next search strategy

The additional listed-company and restructuring disclosures suggest a clear research method:

> **Do not search only for what Yooli itself disclosed. Search for what Yooli's partners were compelled to disclose when they entered listed-company transactions, financings, M&A, or court proceedings.**

Priorities include:

1. full-text searching for Beijing Honghe Boji Financial Information Service Co., Ltd. / Beijing Honghe Boji Information Technology Co., Ltd. in listed-company announcements and major-asset-restructuring materials;
2. locating more external asset channels for which cooperation limits, outstanding balances, and compensation scales can be quantified;
3. checking whether known partners experienced capital reduction, dishonest-debtor status, freezes, judicial auctions, license problems, or loss of compensation capacity in 2019–2021;
4. aligning those partner risk timelines with Yooli's own sharp increases in compensation and overdue amounts.

This approach currently produces more information than continuing to infer functions from the names of Yooli's directly invested corporate subsidiaries.
