# Shareholder Relationship Between Wu Yiran and Ren Yong, and the 2018–2019 Corporate-Governance Disputes

> Compiled: 2026-10-17  
> Chinese original: [`吴逸然任用股东关系及2018至2019公司治理纠纷.md`](../docs/吴逸然任用股东关系及2018至2019公司治理纠纷.md)  
> Sources: public court decisions from China Judgments Online, currently available Yooli corporate-registration and equity materials, and contemporaneous 2019 reporting on P2P filing / transformation policy.  
> The purpose of this article is not to decide which litigant “was telling the truth,” but to reconstruct a previously underappreciated fact: **during Yooli’s operating entity’s 2018 capital increase, 2019 upward equity restructuring, and subsequent capital increase, Ren Yong later expressly denied signing or authorizing several key corporate documents and entered into continuing litigation with companies in the Honghe Boji group over name rights, company registration, and the validity of shareholder resolutions.**
>
> **Important evidentiary boundary:** the public court decisions located so far did **not** find that Wu Yiran forged Ren Yong’s signature, and no merits judgment in the materials reviewed has determined whether Ren’s signatures on the 2018 shareholder resolution or the 2019 equity-transfer documents were genuine or false. Ren’s assertions are litigant allegations. In the resolution-validity case, the courts principally dismissed the action on standing grounds because Ren was no longer a registered shareholder at the time and his status as supervisor did not independently confer the required standing; the appellate court affirmed. Therefore, “Ren denied the signature” must not be rewritten as “the court found the signature forged.”  
> This English version is a research translation. If wording differs, the Chinese original controls.

## 1. Executive summary

The currently available public materials establish at least the following:

1. **Yooli’s operating entity underwent an important capital increase in 2018.** A shareholder resolution dated 2018-04-01 in the corporate-registration file recorded an increase of registered capital to **RMB 500 million**, with Wu Yiran subscribing approximately **RMB 430.52 million** and Ren Yong approximately **RMB 69.48 million**. The related registration changes were completed in May 2018.
2. **Ren Yong later expressly denied that the “Ren Yong” signature on that shareholder resolution was his and denied authorizing another person to sign for him.** He alleged that he had not attended the meeting and argued that the capital increase expanded his subscribed amount to roughly ten times its previous level, potentially increasing his future obligation to make capital contributions.
3. **On 2019-06-26, the registration file contained an equity-transfer agreement and another shareholder resolution.** Those documents recorded Wu and Ren transferring all of their equity in Yooli’s operating entity to **Beijing Honghe Boji Technology Co., Ltd.**, after which the operating entity became 100% owned by that holding company.
4. **Ren likewise denied the authenticity of his signatures on the 2019 equity-transfer agreement and related shareholder resolution and said he did not know about the transfer.**
5. **On 2019-06-28, the operating entity increased registered capital again, from RMB 500 million to RMB 1 billion.** Contemporary media reporting said paid-in capital at that time remained **RMB 50 million**. This “equity move upward + doubling of registered capital” occurred during a period in which proposed P2P filing-pilot capital requirements were widely circulating and platforms across the sector were carrying out capital increases.
6. Public court decisions from 2019–2022 show multiple disputes between Ren Yong and companies in the Honghe Boji system involving **rights to one’s name and reputation, requests to change company registration, and confirmation of the validity of company resolutions**, among other issues.
7. The public decisions obtained so far **did not resolve the central signature-authenticity question on the merits.**

Accordingly, the 2018–2019 changes in Yooli’s corporate structure should not be understood only as ordinary intragroup restructuring. At minimum, Ren’s later litigation shows that **the 2018 capital increase, the 2019 upward equity restructuring, and the P2P filing/capital environment were occurring at the same time, while Ren expressly disputed the authenticity of some key corporate documents.**

## 2. Companies and persons involved

Three companies must be distinguished:

- **北京弘合柏基金融信息服务有限责任公司 — Beijing Honghe Boji Financial Information Service Co., Ltd.**: Yooli’s core operating entity;
- **北京弘合柏基科技有限责任公司 — Beijing Honghe Boji Technology Co., Ltd.**: became the operating entity’s 100% shareholder after the 2019 equity restructuring;
- **弘合柏基（北京）网络技术有限公司 — Honghe Boji (Beijing) Network Technology Co., Ltd.**: appeared in separate name/reputation litigation with Ren Yong and should not be conflated with the first two entities.

Before the 2019 upward equity transfer, Wu Yiran and Ren Yong directly held the operating entity. Based on the subscribed amounts recorded in public corporate materials and court decisions after the 2018 increase:

| Shareholder | Subscribed capital after 2018 increase | Approx. share of registered capital |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Wu Yiran | RMB 430,521,428.6 | 86.10% |
| Ren Yong | RMB 69,478,571.4 | 13.90% |
| Total | RMB 500,000,000 | 100% |

The 2019-06-26 equity-transfer documents in the corporate file moved all of both individuals’ equity into Beijing Honghe Boji Technology Co., Ltd., changing the operating entity from direct natural-person ownership to a wholly corporate-owned structure.

Two days later, on 2019-06-28, the operating entity’s registered capital increased from **RMB 500 million to RMB 1 billion**. Media reporting at the same time stated that the platform’s **paid-in capital remained RMB 50 million**.

## 3. The 2018 capital-increase dispute: Ren Yong denied signing the shareholder resolution

### 3.1 The 2018-04-01 shareholder resolution in the registration file

Haidian District People’s Court **(2021) Jing 0108 Min Chu No. 23134** and Beijing First Intermediate People’s Court **(2022) Jing 01 Min Zhong No. 3451** both referred to a “Beijing Honghe Boji Company Shareholder Resolution” contained in the corporate-registration file.

The document recorded that:

- a shareholder meeting was held on 2018-04-01;
- two shareholders were expected and two were recorded as present;
- registered capital was increased to **RMB 500 million**;
- Wu Yiran subscribed **RMB 430,521,428.6**;
- Ren Yong subscribed **RMB 69,478,571.4**;
- the amended articles of association were approved;
- the document bore signature markings reading “Wu Yiran” and “Ren Yong.”

The company completed the related registration change in May 2018.

### 3.2 Ren Yong’s litigation position

Ren expressly stated that:

- he did not attend the purported shareholder meeting on 2018-04-01;
- the “Ren Yong” signature on the resolution was not written by him;
- he did not authorize another person to sign on his behalf;
- he did not later ratify any substitute signature.

On appeal, Ren further argued that the resolution expanded the amount he had previously subscribed and paid in to approximately **ten times** the earlier amount, potentially exposing him to roughly nine times that amount in additional unpaid subscribed-capital liability. If the company later became insolvent or bankrupt, he argued, the change could directly affect his personal interests.

This is why the litigation matters to the Yooli case: the dispute was not merely about the form of an internal signature. It concerned a **substantial expansion of potential shareholder capital-contribution liability**.

### 3.3 The company’s response

Yooli’s operating entity disputed Ren’s account. The decisions record the company’s position that the signature should be treated as Ren’s own. The company said Ren did not attend the meeting on April 1, but that the resolution was mailed to him, signed by him, and returned to the filing agent.

The factual positions therefore directly conflict:

> **Ren: I did not attend, did not sign, and did not authorize anyone.**  
> **Company: Ren did not attend, but the document was mailed to him and he personally signed and returned it.**

The public decisions obtained so far did not decide that factual conflict on the merits.

## 4. The 2019 P2P filing-related capital-increase wave: what did RMB 500 million and RMB 1 billion mean?

This policy background matters because otherwise Yooli’s 2019 increase from RMB 500 million to RMB 1 billion can look like an isolated corporate decision.

### 4.1 The filing-pilot proposal circulating in April 2019 centered on RMB 500 million paid-in capital for nationwide platforms

In April 2019, a document titled *Conditional Filing Pilot Work Plan for Online Lending Information Intermediaries* circulated widely in the industry. Financial media reported that the draft contemplated:

- platforms operating within a single provincial region: paid-in registered capital of at least **RMB 50 million**;
- nationwide platforms: paid-in registered capital of at least **RMB 500 million**;
- capital to consist of monetary funds and shareholders’ own funds;
- a period within which capital would need to be supplemented.

The proposal **was not formally and publicly confirmed by regulators as a final nationwide rule** at the time. It therefore cannot be described as a formally effective national regulation. However, contemporaneous media widely documented a sector-wide capital-increase wave driven by filing expectations, with a number of leading P2P platforms increasing registered capital to RMB 500 million or RMB 1 billion or more in order to signal capital strength and filing ambition.

Sources:

- JRJ / 01Caijing, *Major News: Full P2P Filing Pilot Plan Leaked*:  
  https://bank.jrj.com.cn/2019/04/09164427354891.shtml
- China News Service, *P2P Filing Push Sees Some Platforms Increase Capital Fivefold*:  
  https://www.chinanews.com/cj/2019/05-15/8836753.shtml
- Qingdao News, *Number of Platforms Halved in a Year as Filing Pilot Enters Sprint Phase*:  
  https://finance.qingdaonews.com/content/2019-06/13/content_20408427.htm

Yooli’s June 2019 increase of registered capital to RMB 1 billion therefore fits very closely with the sector-wide window of filing-related capital strengthening.

However, no internal Yooli board materials, regulatory correspondence, or statement from Wu Yiran has been obtained proving that “this increase was specifically intended to satisfy a formal RMB 1 billion P2P filing requirement.” The proper statement is that the timing was **highly related to the regulatory-capital environment**, not that direct causation has been proven.

### 4.2 A formal RMB 1 billion registered-capital requirement later appeared in transformation rules — in November 2019

In November 2019, the relevant rectification offices issued the *Guiding Opinions on Pilot Transformation of Online Lending Information Intermediaries into Microloan Companies* (often called “Document No. 83”). Those rules stated that a platform transforming into a nationwide microloan company should have:

- registered capital of at least **RMB 1 billion**;
- first-stage paid-in monetary capital of at least **RMB 500 million**;
- and first-stage paid-in capital no less than **one tenth of the platform’s outstanding P2P loan balance at the time of transformation**.

Sources:

- Fujian local financial authority republication of the full text:  
  https://fjjrb.gov.cn/zcfg/gzbwgfxwj/202008/t20200813_5353548.htm
- China Securities Journal, *P2P-to-Microloan Transformation Plan Released*:  
  https://www.cs.com.cn/yh/04/201911/t20191128_6003265.html

The chronology matters: **Yooli had already increased registered capital to RMB 1 billion in June 2019, before the November rule formally introduced the RMB 1 billion nationwide-microloan threshold.**

The more accurate description is therefore:

> **By the first half of 2019, P2P filing-pilot expectations had already turned capital strength into a key threshold for leading platforms; a widely circulated proposal contemplated RMB 500 million paid-in capital for nationwide operations and contributed to an industry capital-increase wave. Yooli raised registered capital from RMB 500 million to RMB 1 billion during that window, although paid-in capital was still only RMB 50 million. Only in November 2019 did formal transformation rules clearly require RMB 1 billion registered capital and RMB 500 million first-stage paid-in capital for nationwide microloan transformation.**

Registered capital, subscribed capital, paid-in capital, and actual cash on hand must therefore be kept strictly separate.

## 5. The 2019 upward equity restructuring: Ren also denied signing the transfer documents

The resolution-validity case disclosed another important group of registration documents.

### 5.1 The transfer agreement and shareholder resolution in the registration file

The court decisions record a transfer agreement providing that:

- Ren Yong would transfer equity corresponding to **RMB 69,478,571.4** of subscribed capital in Yooli’s operating entity to Beijing Honghe Boji Technology Co., Ltd.;
- Beijing Honghe Boji Technology would acquire that interest;
- the transfer would take formal effect on **2019-06-26**;
- from the transfer date, Ren would cease to enjoy shareholder rights and bear shareholder obligations for that equity, with those rights and obligations passing to the holding company.

A shareholder resolution from the same period stated that:

- Beijing Honghe Boji Technology would be added as the new shareholder;
- Wu Yiran and Ren Yong would withdraw as direct shareholders;
- Wu would transfer equity corresponding to approximately RMB 430.52 million of capital to the holding company;
- Ren would transfer equity corresponding to approximately RMB 69.48 million;
- the company’s articles would be amended.

The documents carried signature markings reading “Wu Yiran” and “Ren Yong” and became the basis for the upper-level ownership structure visible today.

### 5.2 Ren denied knowledge and signature

Ren again stated in litigation that:

- he did not sign the transfer agreement or shareholder resolution;
- he did not accept the authenticity of the signatures attributed to him;
- he had not known about the equity transfer.

The appellate ruling also records Ren submitting additional case materials in an attempt to show that he had separately brought litigation concerning the alleged “forged signature and transfer of all his equity to a third party.”

The appellate court did not use those materials to decide the authenticity of the transfer documents. Instead, it held that the new materials did not change the standing analysis in the resolution-validity proceeding.

The established position is therefore:

> **The 2019 upward equity transfer did occur and was registered; Ren later expressly denied having signed or known about it; and the public decisions obtained in this proceeding did not determine whether the disputed signatures were genuine.**

## 6. From 2019 through 2022, this was not a single lawsuit but a series of disputes

The original China Judgments Online pages preserved by the project establish at least the following cases:

| Case | Parties / cause of action | What can be established |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **(2019) Su 01 Min Xia Zhong No. 984** | Honghe Boji (Beijing) Network Technology Co., Ltd. and Ren Yong; rights to name and reputation | **Ren was the original plaintiff and the company the original defendant.** Ren alleged that the company had used his name to register him as a shareholder, infringing his name and reputation rights. The company appealed only the jurisdiction ruling. Nanjing Intermediate Court kept jurisdiction in Nanjing. This was not a merits determination of infringement. |
| **(2020) Su 01 Min Xia Zhong No. 111** | Beijing Honghe Boji Technology Co., Ltd. and Ren Yong; rights to name and reputation | **Ren again was the original plaintiff and the company the original defendant.** The decision records Ren alleging that Honghe Boji Technology had used his name to register him as a company shareholder and thereby infringed his rights. The company appealed the jurisdiction ruling; Nanjing Intermediate Court rejected that appeal. Again, the ruling did not establish the underlying infringement. |
| **(2020) Jing 0108 Min Chu No. 22556** | Ren Yong v. Beijing Honghe Boji Financial Information Service Co., Ltd., request to change company registration; Beijing Honghe Boji Technology as third party | Ren later applied to withdraw the action; Haidian District Court permitted withdrawal. No merits judgment resulted. |
| **(2021) Jing 0108 Min Chu No. 23134** | Ren Yong v. Beijing Honghe Boji Financial Information Service Co., Ltd., dispute over confirmation of validity of company resolution | Ren sought confirmation that the 2018-04-01 shareholder resolution did not exist / was invalid. Haidian District Court dismissed because he was no longer a registered shareholder and supervisor status did not independently confer standing for that claim. |
| **(2022) Jing 01 Min Zhong No. 3451** | Ren Yong appeal, resolution-validity dispute | Beijing First Intermediate Court affirmed on standing grounds and did not determine the authenticity of signatures on the 2018 resolution or 2019 transfer documents. |

The cases show conflict between Ren and the Honghe Boji system lasting for years and spanning:

- use of his name;
- shareholder registration;
- equity transfer;
- capital increases;
- validity of shareholder resolutions;
- changes to corporate registration.

One point must be corrected carefully: **the five public decisions do not show Honghe Boji companies initiating substantive lawsuits against Ren.** Although the captions of the two Nanjing appellate jurisdiction cases list the companies first, the body of each decision shows Ren was the first-instance plaintiff and the company the first-instance defendant; the company was appealing only the jurisdiction ruling. The remaining cases were also initiated or appealed by Ren’s side.

The strongest formulation supported by the record is therefore:

> **Around 2019, Ren Yong and the Honghe Boji corporate system led and controlled by Wu Yiran were already involved in sustained and substantive disputes concerning corporate governance, identity, and equity. In the public decisions currently available, the litigation was mainly initiated from Ren’s side.**

## 7. Why did the courts not decide whether the signatures were genuine?

A common misreading is to see Ren repeatedly say “that is not my signature” and then rewrite the record as “the court confirmed that someone forged Ren’s signature.” The public decisions do not support that.

In **(2021) Jing 0108 Min Chu No. 23134**, Haidian District Court held that:

- according to corporate-registration records, Ren was no longer a registered shareholder when he filed the case;
- although he remained registered as a company supervisor, the Company Law’s supervisory powers mainly concern directors and senior management and did not automatically give him standing as supervisor to seek a declaration that the shareholder resolution had not been validly formed;
- Ren therefore was not a proper plaintiff for that action, so the case was dismissed procedurally.

Beijing First Intermediate Court in **(2022) Jing 01 Min Zhong No. 3451** substantially followed the same approach.

Ren submitted materials from other cases on appeal and argued that they showed he had continuously disputed the equity transfer and signatures. The court held that these materials still did not establish his standing in the particular case and did not change the procedural result.

The correct evidentiary conclusion is therefore:

- **Ren expressly denied the signatures and authorization;**
- **the company expressly maintained that the signatures were genuine;**
- **the courts did not decide which account was true in these rulings;**
- **dismissal of Ren’s action does not mean the courts found the signatures genuine.**

## 8. Why do these disputes matter to analysis of the Yooli case?

### 8.1 The 2019 “upward equity transfer” cannot be treated only as routine intragroup restructuring

Looking only at corporate registration, the main change in 2019 was:

```text
Wu Yiran + Ren Yong direct ownership
        ↓
Beijing Honghe Boji Technology Co., Ltd. 100% ownership
        ↓
Beijing Honghe Boji Financial Information Service Co., Ltd. (Yooli operating entity)
```

Without litigation materials this could look like an ordinary holding-company reorganization by the founders.

The court decisions add a major fact: **Ren later expressly denied signing or knowing about at least some of the key documents underlying that change.**

Accordingly, research on the 2019 corporate structure must separate “the registration change was completed” from “all original shareholders accepted the change.”

### 8.2 The 2018–2019 capital changes overlapped closely with the regulatory window

Registered capital rose to RMB 500 million in 2018 and then to RMB 1 billion in June 2019. The latter timing fell squarely within the period when filing-pilot capital thresholds were widely circulating and leading P2P platforms were increasing registered capital.

The governance disputes therefore cannot be understood wholly apart from the industry’s regulatory setting: **Yooli was trying to demonstrate stronger capital capacity and preserve continuing-operation or transformation options externally, while internally a shareholder was disputing the authenticity of documents concerning capital increases and equity changes.**

The evidence still does not establish the precise internal motive of Wu Yiran or the company for any particular change in 2018 or 2019.

### 8.3 Registered, subscribed, and paid-in capital must be kept separate

Media reporting in June 2019 said Yooli’s registered capital had reached **RMB 1 billion**, while paid-in capital remained only **RMB 50 million**.

Therefore:

- RMB 1 billion registered capital does not mean the company had RMB 1 billion of cash on its balance sheet;
- registered capital cannot be used directly to estimate funds currently available for wind-down;
- unpaid subscribed-capital obligations may still matter under particular legal circumstances and should not be ignored.

Ren’s challenge to the authenticity of some capital-increase documents makes that potential liability chain more complicated than in an ordinary company.

### 8.4 At minimum there was an obvious conflict of interest and governance between the Wu-led corporate system and Ren Yong

The litigation does not establish the state of their private personal relationship, but it does establish that:

- Wu Yiran was a core manager and legal representative within the Honghe Boji corporate system;
- Ren repeatedly disputed the use of his name, his shareholder status, equity transfers, and capital-contribution obligations reflected in corporate documents;
- multiple public court decisions record that conflict in formal judicial materials.

Accordingly, when evaluating whether shareholder resources might later be coordinated to supplement wind-down funding, it is not enough to consider only the two individuals’ financial resources. One must also consider **whether the shareholder group could actually reach a consistent interest and contribution arrangement.**

That is a governance-risk factor only; it does not prove that either party would refuse to participate in Yooli’s wind-down.

## 9. Key gaps that public evidence cannot currently fill

This line of investigation has moved beyond rumor because it is supported by multiple public court decisions. However, the following high-value materials have not been located in public sources and should not be treated as ordinary “to-do items” likely to be found by more web searching:

1. **the original 2018-04-01 shareholder resolution or high-resolution corporate-registration archive copy**, to examine signatures, document formation, and filing timing;
2. **the original 2019-06-26 transfer agreement and related shareholder resolution**, to examine signatures/seals, transfer price, and related arrangements;
3. **the complete June 2019 filing package for increasing registered capital from RMB 500 million to RMB 1 billion**, including shareholder decisions, articles amendments, and contribution arrangements;
4. **merits outcomes in any separate litigation brought by Ren concerning the equity transfer or signature authenticity**, plus first-instance merits decisions in the two name/reputation cases;
5. **complete corporate-registration archives for Beijing Honghe Boji Technology Co., Ltd., including historical shareholding and paid-in-capital materials**;
6. **capital-contribution bank records, capital-verification materials, financial records, or related audit workpapers**;
7. **internal or regulatory materials concerning Yooli’s communications with local regulators about filing, capital increases, or transformation.**

Most of those materials would require **access to market-regulation archives, litigation files, audit workpapers, regulatory files, or internal company records**. Public internet sources generally reveal only registration results, summaries of court decisions, or peripheral reporting.

The project therefore does not treat these items as ordinary tasks that can reliably be completed through open web searches and does not fill the gaps with inference. If any become available through newly published judgments, corporate-file access, or materials released by parties, this article should be updated accordingly.
